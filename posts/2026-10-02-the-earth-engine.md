## §0 Sammanfattning

*Solen tänder, allt annat är nedströms.*

Earth can be read as one engine fired by the Sun, in which climate, life, minds and institutions are successive stages, each running on charge compressed by the stage above it **[D]**. In that reading the failures that look unrelated, a weakening ocean current, a trophic cascade, burnout, a psychotic episode, poverty, famine, regulatory capture, failure to act, crime and war, sort into a short list of engine faults: a starved fuel line, a timing fault, a captured governor, a failed cooling loop, a stall, a leak **[D]**. One part of this is more than metaphor: transitions in several of these systems share measurable early-warning signals, so the classification makes testable predictions **[S]**. The post does not claim one mechanism behind these failures, does not offer a clinical model of any mental illness, and does not assign blame to anyone a failure happens to; each failure keeps its own science, and the engine is a device for sorting them **[D]**.

## §1 The power source

Living systems maintain their order by exporting entropy, feeding, in Schrödinger's phrase, on "negative entropy" rather than on energy as such (Schrödinger 1944) **[E]**. For the planet, the gradient is between incoming sunlight, emitted by a surface near 5800 K, and outgoing infrared radiated to space at a much lower temperature; the Earth system converts part of that gradient into work, and life is one of the processes that does so (Kleidon 2010) **[S]**. The atmosphere's general circulation is itself maintained by conversion of available potential energy, created by uneven heating, into the kinetic energy of winds (Lorenz 1955) **[E]**.

So the Sun is the power source and climate and life are downstream, in the sense that energy flows one way: down the gradient **[E]**. Control does not flow one way. Life has rewritten the atmosphere it runs in, and minds are now rewriting the climate that fires them; Kleidon (2010) treats life's feedback on the Earth system's thermodynamics as central **[S]**. The engine is fired from above and steered, increasingly, from below **[D]**.

## §2 Five stages

| Stage | What it compresses | What it burns | Exhaust |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sun | Gravity into fusion | Hydrogen | Light |
| Climate | Uneven heating into available potential energy | The equator-to-pole gradient | Infrared to space |
| Life | Light into biomass | Biomass, at about 10% transfer per trophic level | CO₂, heat, detritus |
| Mind | Food into a working brain | Glucose, at about 20% of resting energy for 2% of body mass | Heat, behaviour |
| Culture | Minds into institutions, symbols and markets | Attention, labour, trust | Records, marks, emissions |

The life row rests on Lindeman's trophic-dynamic analysis, which established the progressive loss of energy at each transfer between levels (Lindeman 1942) **[E]**. The mind row rests on the measured energy budget of the human brain (Raichle & Gusnard 2002) **[E]**. The culture row is the corpus's reading **[D]**.

At every stage what burns is not matter, which is recycled, but form: stored low-entropy structure **[D]**. A body is compressed charge; a trained mind is compressed charge; an institution is compressed charge **[D]**. The bite and the spark of [The Bite](/posts/the-bite/) (DRK-184) are the ignition events that open stored form and release it **[D]**.

### §2.1 The brand stage [E/D]

*Brand* is Old English for fire, flame, a burning piece of wood; the "mark made by a hot iron" dates from the 1550s and "a particular make of goods" from 1854 (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. Its PIE root *\*gwher-* "to heat" is the same root as Greek *thermē*, the root of *thermal* (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. So *brand* and *thermodynamics* are cousins **[E]**. Swedish *utbränd* and English *outbranded* share the burning root **[E]**. Read as a stage, a brand is the culture stage's ignition: a mark burnt into minds, whose exhaust is the scar it leaves **[D]**.

## §3 Formal sketch: stocks, gates and debts (analogy, not derivation)

Index the stages by $k = 1, \dots, 5$. Let $S_k(t) \ge 0$ be the stock of usable form at stage $k$ (biomass, a mind's working capacity, an institution's trust), $R_k(t)$ its regeneration rate and $C_k(t)$ its consumption rate, both $\ge 0$. Then

$$\frac{dS_k}{dt} = R_k - C_k .$$

Let $g_k(t) \in [0,1]$ be the **gate**: the fraction of potential ignition that the stage's controls allow at time $t$. In an engine the gate is the throttle and the ignition timing together **[M]**. Define the stage's accumulated deficit

$$D_k(t) = \int_0^t \max\big(0,\; C_k(s) - R_k(s)\big)\, ds, \qquad \dot D_k \ge 0 .$$

This has the same form as the corpus's trophic debt $\Theta$, which integrates the violation of care ([Ouroboros Debt](/posts/the-ouroboros-debt/), DRK-176), and the same form as coherence debt $K(t)$ ([The Coherence Debt](/posts/the-coherence-debt/), DRK-121) **[D]**. Whether $D_k$ is an instance of $\Theta$, rather than merely shaped like it, is open **[H]**. That would require showing that over-consumption of a stage is a destruction of exogenous variety in the sense of the care operator.

**The earth engine** (proposed corpus term): the reading of the Earth system as a cascade of heat engines fired by the Sun, in which each stage compresses usable form from the stage above, burns it through a gate, and fails in one of a small number of engine fault modes **[D]**.

Many of the systems in §5–§7 have alternative stable states, and theory and data show that as such a system approaches a tipping point it recovers more slowly from small perturbations, which appears as rising variance and autocorrelation in its fluctuations (Scheffer et al. 2009) **[S]**. This is what lets the engine reading make predictions rather than only describe: if a stage's stock is being run down toward a threshold, the same statistical signature should appear before the failure, whatever the stage **[D]**.

## §4 Six engine faults

| Fault | In an engine | Earth-engine reading |
|---|---|---|
| Starvation | Lean mixture or blocked fuel line: power falls, heat rises | Charge exists but does not reach the cylinder |
| Timing | Knock or pre-ignition: combustion out of phase with the piston | Ignition at the wrong moment or on the wrong target |
| Captured governor | The regulator coupled to what it should limit | Control serving the throttle it was built to restrain |
| Cooling failure | Heat not carried away: the engine runs hot | A heat-transport loop weakens or switches state |
| Stall or seizure | No cycle: load exceeds torque, or parts lock | No ignition although charge is present |
| Blow-by | Pressure leaks past the rings, outside the useful stroke | Work extracted around the channel meant for it |

The table is the corpus's construction **[D]**. Three words in it have roots worth noting.

*Govern* is Latin *gubernare* "to steer", borrowed from Greek *kybernan*, to pilot a ship, the root of *cybernetics* (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. Maxwell's paper "On governors" founded the mathematical theory of the feedback devices that hold an engine's speed (Maxwell 1868) **[E]**. Government and the engine governor are the same word for the same job **[E/D]**.

*Seize* entered English as a legal term for taking possession, and by about 1300 meant "to grip with the hands or teeth" (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. The sense "sudden attack" of an illness is attested from 1779 (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. So the word for the bite also names two opposite failures: the brain's seizure, runaway synchronous firing, and the engine's seizure, locked parts and no firing at all **[D]**.

The [Damper and the Wastegate](/posts/the-damper-and-the-wastegate/) (DRK-188) already read a cooling failure, an overheated damper that stopped damping, as the governing problem of systems that do work **[D]**. This post extends that diagnosis from one machine to the cascade **[D]**.

## §5 Planetary and ecological stages

### §5.1 Ocean currents: the cooling loop

The Atlantic meridional overturning circulation carries heat northward and is one of the climate's main heat-transport loops **[E]**. Stommel's two-box model showed that circulation driven by temperature and salinity differences can have two stable regimes of flow (Stommel 1961) **[E]**. The AMOC is listed among the climate's tipping elements (Lenton et al. 2008) **[S]**. A temperature fingerprint consistent with a weakening of about 15% since the mid-twentieth century has been reported (Caesar et al. 2018) **[S]**. A statistical analysis of early-warning indicators projected a possible collapse within this century (Ditlevsen & Ditlevsen 2023) **[H]**; its timing is disputed in the literature.

Engine reading: a cooling failure with a switch in it **[D]**. A cooling loop that can sit in two states is a thermostat that can stick, and a stuck thermostat moves the hot spot rather than removing it **[D]**.

### §5.2 Trophic cascades: the damper removed

The loss of apex consumers sets off cascades through lower trophic levels, with documented effects on vegetation, disease, wildfire, carbon storage and water quality across ecosystems (Estes et al. 2011) **[S]**. Engine reading: an apex consumer is a damper on the herbivore oscillation, and removing it lets the stage below run without its governor **[D]**. This is the case Lindeman's energetics alone do not predict: the apex carries little of the energy flow, and almost all of the control **[D]**.

## §6 The mind stage

This section maps the shape of failures onto engine faults. It is not a clinical account, it says nothing about worth or blame, and none of it is guidance for anyone's treatment **[D]**.

### §6.1 Burnout: running lean

The WHO's ICD-11 lists burn-out as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed, and explicitly not as a medical condition (World Health Organization 2019) **[E]**. McEwen's concept of allostatic load describes how stress mediators that protect in the short run cause wear when they are engaged too often or for too long (McEwen 1998) **[S]**. Engine reading: a stage where $C_k > R_k$ for long enough that $D_k$ grows; the engine still runs, hot and weak, until it does not **[D]**. *Utbränd* names the fault exactly **[M]**.

### §6.2 Depression: limp mode?

Nesse argued that some forms of low mood may be adaptive responses that disengage effort from unreachable goals, while being clear that this does not make clinical depression beneficial (Nesse 2000) **[H]**. Modern engines have a limp-home mode that cuts power to protect the engine when a sensor reports a fault **[E]**. If Nesse's hypothesis holds for some cases, those cases would be limp mode: a protective gate closing, which can misfire and stay closed long after the fault is gone **[H]**. Separately and more firmly, in patient time series rising autocorrelation and variance in emotion ratings were reported before transitions into and out of depression, the same critical-slowing signature seen in climate and ecosystems (Van de Leemput et al. 2014) **[S]**.

### §6.3 Psychosis: a timing fault

Kapur proposed that psychosis involves aberrant assignment of salience, with dopamine signalling marking irrelevant stimuli as significant, and delusions arising as explanations of that experience (Kapur 2003) **[S]**. Engine reading: ignition fires on the wrong stroke and the wrong target; the charge burns, but out of phase with the cycle that would turn it into work **[D]**. The mapping concerns mechanism shape only; it says nothing about the person, whose experience is real and whose reasoning about it is often coherent given the misplaced salience **[D]**.

## §7 The culture stage

### §7.1 Poverty: the fuel is taxed before it burns

In field and laboratory studies, financial scarcity reduced performance on cognitive tasks, which the authors read as scarcity consuming mental bandwidth (Mani et al. 2013) **[S]**. The size and generality of the effect have been debated since **[H]**. Engine reading: poverty is not a property of a cylinder but a fuel-line restriction at the culture stage that also taxes the mind stage's charge **[D]**.

### §7.2 Famine: a closed fuel line

Sen showed that major famines, including the Bengal famine of 1943, occurred without a correspondingly large decline in total food availability, and explained them as collapses in people's entitlements to food (Sen 1981) **[S]**. Engine reading: starvation with fuel in the tank, a fuel-line fault at the distribution gate **[D]**. This is the clearest case for the claim that most earth-engine failures are gate failures, not supply failures **[D]**.

### §7.3 Regulatory capture: the governor coupled to the throttle

Stigler argued that regulation is, as a rule, acquired by the industry and designed and operated primarily for its benefit (Stigler 1971) **[S]**. Engine reading: a captured governor no longer limits engine speed; it opens the throttle on demand of the cylinder it was meant to restrain **[D]**. It is the most dangerous fault in the table, because a captured governor still reports that the engine is governed **[D]**.

### §7.4 Failure to act: the stall

Hardin argued that individually rational use of a shared resource drives it to ruin (Hardin 1968) **[S]**. Ostrom documented many communities that govern commons successfully through local institutions, which shows the tragedy is not inevitable (Ostrom 1990) **[E]**. In bystander experiments, the likelihood of helping in an emergency fell as the number of other presumed witnesses rose (Darley & Latané 1968) **[S]**. Engine reading: charge present, ignition withheld because each cylinder waits for another to fire; the stall **[D]**. Ostrom's commons are engines with working local ignition **[D]**.

### §7.5 Crime: blow-by

Using US county data, Ranson estimated that higher temperatures are associated with more violent crime (Ranson 2014) **[S]**. Engine reading, as a pointer only: work extracted around the channel built for it, pressure leaking past the rings **[M]**. The causes of crime are social, economic and individual, and a heat correlation is one input among them **[D]**.

### §7.6 War: detonation

A meta-analysis reported that deviations toward warmer temperatures and more extreme rainfall are associated with increased interpersonal and intergroup conflict (Hsiang et al. 2013) **[H]**. A large group of conflict researchers disputed the strength and generality of that conclusion (Buhaug et al. 2014) **[H]**. Engine reading: knock, the compressed charge igniting at many points at once, producing pressure peaks that destroy the engine instead of driving it **[D]**. Whatever the climate link, the structural point is that war burns the highest-value charge of every stage at once, minds included **[D]**.

## §8 Propagation and limits

Faults travel across stages **[D]**. A cooling-loop switch (§5.1) moves rainfall; moved rainfall changes harvests; a harvest shock becomes a famine if the distribution gate fails (§7.2); famine and heat raise the pressure in which knock (§7.6) becomes likely **[D]**. Faults also travel upward: a captured governor (§7.3) keeps emissions high, which works on the cooling loop **[D]**. The engine is fired from above and fails in both directions **[D]**.

There is a lesson from accident investigation in this. [The Survivable Glitch](/posts/the-survivable-glitch/) (DRK-138) read the *haveriutredning* as the discipline that names the failed component without stopping at the person nearest to it **[D]**. Applied here, the question for each fault is which gate failed, not which cylinder to blame **[D]**. The predictive point of [The Stal King](/posts/the-stal-king/) (DRK-192) carries over as well: ignition timing is computed ahead of the piston, and every stage that times its spark late burns its charge into heat **[D]**.

Anti-totalisation applies with full force. The engine is a classification device; it does not supply a mechanism for any of the failures, and two failures in the same fault class can have entirely different causes **[D]**. A framework that tried to hold all of this in one central gaze would commit the Beholder's error from [The Stal King](/posts/the-stal-king/) (DRK-192) **[D]**.

## §9 Falsification (DRK-131)

**F1: Shared early warning.** §3 predicts that a stage approaching a fault transition shows critical slowing down regardless of stage. **Refuted** if, across well-measured transitions in at least three stages (for example ocean circulation, ecosystem state shifts, mood transitions), rising autocorrelation and variance precede transitions no more often than in matched periods without a transition.

**F2: Gate before supply.** §7.2 and §8 claim that most earth-engine failures are gate failures. **Refuted** if, in famine records, food-availability decline accounts for the large majority of major famines, so that entitlement collapse adds little explanatory power.

**F3: Captured governor.** §7.3 predicts that a regulator's coupling to the regulated industry predicts outcomes favouring the industry. **Refuted** if measures of that coupling, such as personnel flow and funding dependence, show no relation to rule outcomes across regulatory agencies.

**F4: Scope limit.** The engine table (§4) is a classification, not a causal theory. The mental-health readings (§6) concern the shape of mechanisms and carry no clinical or moral claim. The etymologies in §2.1 and §4 are illustrations, removable without loss to the argument. If F1–F3 fail, §1 and §2 remain as established energetics, and the six-fault taxonomy is withdrawn as a predictive claim and kept, at most, as a teaching device.
