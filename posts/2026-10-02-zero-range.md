## §0 Sammanfattning

*Människan saknade klor, så hon lärde sig kasta dem.*

*Range* is, at its root, a ring around a point; free range, weapon range and zero range are three radii of the same circle, and the last of them is the point itself **[E/D]**. Nearly every animal that kills does so at or near zero range, with teeth, claws and armour, and the few animals with projectiles reach centimetres or a few metres, mostly to stun, blind or deter **[S]**. Humans are poorly armed at zero range and became the only animal that kills reliably from tens of metres, by throwing and then by artefacts that throw for them **[S]**. The post reads ranged weapons as compensation for missing claws, fangs and armour, and distance itself as a variable that lowers the threshold for killing **[D]**. It does not claim that range explains human violence, nor that the word chain in §1 proves anything; the roots are tagged one by one **[D]**.

## §1 The chain, root by root

*Range* comes from Old French *reng* "row, rank", from a Frankish word for a ring or circle, from the PIE root *\*sker-* "to turn, bend"; the sense "area over which animals seek food" dates from the 1620s (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. *Orange* came from Sanskrit through Persian *nārang* and Arabic *nāranj*, and the dictionary attributes the loss of its initial *n-* probably to the article, *a narange* becoming *an orange* (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. Reading *orange* as *O range*, zero range, is therefore a cut at the same seam where the word was once cut for real **[M]**.

*Melee* is French *mêlée*, from *mesler* "to mix, mingle", the root also of *meddle* and *medley* (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. *Salient* is Latin *salire* "to leap", and its sense "striking" descends from Aristotle's *punctum saliens*, the leaping heart of the embryo (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. *Assault* is the same verb with *ad-*: to leap at **[E]**. So the corpus's earlier pounce ([The Stal King](/posts/the-stal-king/), DRK-192) and the aberrant salience of [The Conviction Gate](/posts/the-conviction-gate/) (DRK-194) stand on one Latin root **[D]**.

*Cramp* descends from a Germanic *\*kramp-* "bent, crooked" (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**; Swedish *kramp* and *krampanfall* belong to the same family **[S]**. *Krampus* is of uncertain origin: one derivation runs from German *Krampen* "claw", another only to the Germanic base of *cramp* (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[H]**. *Claw* is Germanic *\*klawō*, and *clause* is Latin *claudere* "to close"; the two are unrelated (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. *Seize* meant "to grip with hands or teeth" by about 1300, the bite under the seizure of [The Earth Engine](/posts/the-earth-engine/) (DRK-193) (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**.

Two roots for bending meet without being related: *range* from *\*sker-* and *cramp* from *\*kramp-* **[E]**. Two roots for closure meet the same way: *claudere* gives *clause* and *claustrophobia*, Germanic *\*kramp-* gives *cramped* **[E]**. The convergences are the point; the shared sounds (claw, clause, Claus) are pointers only **[M]**.

## §2 Formal sketch: range as a ring (analogy, not derivation)

Let $x$ be a target in a metric space and $B(x, r) = \{\, y : d(x,y) < r \,\}$ the open ball of radius $r$ around it: a ring of reach, the literal sense of *range* **[E]** for the definition. In [The Stal King](/posts/the-stal-king/) (DRK-192) the stalk at $x$ was what survives as $r \to 0$ **[D]**. Zero range is that limit for bodies: the point where attacker and target share a neighbourhood and the encounter becomes a mixture, a *mêlée*, rather than an exchange across space **[D]**.

Let $L(d)$ be the lethality an attacker can deliver at distance $d$, and $E(d)$ the exposure of the attacker to retaliation at that distance **[D]**. For an animal armed with teeth and claws, $L$ is supported near zero: $L(d) \approx 0$ for $d$ beyond body reach, and at that reach $E(d)$ is high, because the prey or rival can bite back **[D]**. Ranged weapons do two things at once: they extend the support of $L$ outward, and they make $E(d)$ fall as $d$ grows **[D]**. The human strategy is the ratio

$$\frac{L(d)}{E(d)} \quad \text{increasing in } d ,$$

harm delivered rising relative to harm risked as distance grows **[D]**.

**Zero range** (proposed corpus term): the limit at which the distance between attacker and target vanishes and the encounter becomes a mixture of bodies rather than an exchange across space **[D]**.

## §3 What animals have at zero range

The predators of the corpus kill at zero range: the cat's pounce and the monitor's bite end where the body meets the body ([The Bite](/posts/the-bite/), DRK-184) **[D]**. The animals that act at a distance do so over short spans and rarely kill that way **[S]**.

Archerfish shoot jets of water to knock insects from overhanging vegetation, and in experiments they learned to down rapidly moving targets, including by watching other fish (Schuster et al. 2006) **[S]**. The prey is knocked into the water and taken there: the projectile delivers it to zero range rather than killing it at a distance **[D]**. Snapping shrimp close an enlarged claw so fast that a cavitation bubble forms and collapses, producing the snap; the effect reaches a few centimetres (Versluis et al. 2000) **[S]**. It is the purest case for this post: the claw itself turned into a short-range projectile weapon **[D]**. Spitting has evolved independently in several cobra lineages as a defence, aimed at the eyes of threats, with venom components that cause intense pain (Kazandjian et al. 2021) **[S]**. It deters at a few metres; it is not a hunting weapon **[S]**.

None of these is a lethal hunting attack over tens of metres **[S]**. Across the animal kingdom, killing is done in contact **[S]**.

## §4 The naked thrower

Humans are poorly armed at zero range: small canines, flat nails rather than claws, no armour, thin and hairless skin **[E]**. What they have instead is the throw **[S]**. Roach and colleagues showed that the human shoulder stores elastic energy for high-speed throwing, and argued from the fossil record that the anatomy enabling it appeared in *Homo* around two million years ago (Roach et al. 2013) **[S]**.

The oldest well-preserved hunting weapons are the wooden spears of Schöningen in Germany, published by Thieme, who read them as throwing spears used in large-game hunting (Thieme 1997) **[S]**. Their age has been revised to about 300,000 years (Richter & Krbetschek 2015) **[S]**. Whether they were thrown or thrust is debated **[H]**. The bow came much later: microresidue and fracture analysis of stone points from Sibudu indicate bow-and-arrow use around 64,000 years ago (Lombard & Phillipson 2010) **[S]**, and bone points from Fa-Hien Lena in Sri Lanka show bow-and-arrow hunting by about 48,000 years ago (Langley et al. 2020) **[S]**.

Read as compensation: the spear is an extended claw, the arrow a detached fang, the shield and armour a carapace carried rather than grown **[D]**. The human who lacked the zero-range body built the body as equipment, and then moved it outward along $d$ **[D]**. The [Earth Engine](/posts/the-earth-engine/) (DRK-193) called fire an exteriorised tooth; the projectile is an exteriorised claw that no longer needs the arm attached to it **[D]**.

## §5 Distance and the gate

Grossman argued that resistance to killing another person falls with physical and psychological distance, from hand-to-hand combat at its highest to bombing and artillery at its lowest (Grossman 1995) **[H]**. His argument rests partly on contested historical data, and it is tagged here as hypothesis **[H]**.

In the terms of [The Conviction Gate](/posts/the-conviction-gate/) (DRK-194), distance is a gate variable **[D]**. At zero range the other is a body, salient in every sense: it leaps at the senses, it bleeds, it looks back **[D]**. At long range the other is a mark, a point on a sight or a screen, and the threshold $\theta$ for acting falls as salience falls **[D]**. That links range to the dehumanisation in [The Weaponised Conviction](/posts/the-weaponised-conviction/) (DRK-195): distance does mechanically what demonising rhetoric does with words, removing the face from the target **[D]**. A weapon that delivers lethality with near-zero exposure and near-zero salience is the limit of the human ratio in §2, and also the limit at which the gate is easiest to open **[D]**.

## §6 The gift and the claw

In Alpine custom, Saint Nicholas, whose Greek name joins *nikē* "victory" and *laos* "people", is accompanied by Krampus, a horned figure who punishes the wicked (Online Etymology Dictionary) **[E]**. Whatever the root of his name, the pair stages the two ends of §2 **[D]**. The giver acts at a distance and leaves gifts unseen in the night; the punisher comes to zero range, to the door, the switch, the claw **[D]**. *Claus* and *claw* share only a sound **[M]**; the custom pairs them anyway, and the pairing is the older claim **[D]**.

## §7 Falsification (DRK-131)

**F1: No lethal animal range.** §3 claims that no non-human animal routinely kills prey or rivals by projectile at ranges of tens of metres. **Refuted** by documentation of any animal species that regularly kills by projectile or thrown object at such distances.

**F2: Compensation.** §4 reads ranged weapons as compensation for weak zero-range armament. **Refuted** if comparative evidence shows that the capacity for high-speed throwing evolved before, not alongside or after, the reduction of canine size and other zero-range armament in the human lineage, in a way that makes compensation an unlikely driver.

**F3: Distance and threshold.** §5 claims that physical distance lowers the threshold for lethal action. **Refuted** if controlled studies, in simulation or in combat records, show no relation between distance to target and willingness to deliver harm once target identity and command pressure are held constant.

**F4: Scope limit.** The word chain in §1 is tagged root by root, and the post survives the removal of every [M] link. Range is offered as one variable among many in the human capacity for violence, not as its explanation. The Nicholas–Krampus reading interprets a custom and tests nothing.

Authorship: Khrug supplied the chain (free range, weapon range, orange as zero range, melee, claws, clausule, Santa Claus, Krampus, cramped, *krampanfall*, assault) and the thesis that ranged weapons are an almost entirely human invention compensating for missing armour, fangs and claws at zero range. Claude (Opus 5.5) drafted the post, the formal sketch, the term **zero range** and the falsifiers. No Clinch review yet.
2. The etymologies of *assault* (from *ad* + *salire*) and *Nicholas* (*nikē* + *laos*) are standard but were not fetched from the dictionary for this post; *salire* itself was. Swedish *kramp* is placed with the Germanic family without a check in SAOB or Hellquist.
3. The roughly two-million-year date for throwing anatomy is Roach et al.'s inference from fossils, not a direct observation. Whether the Schöningen spears were thrown is debated.
4. Grossman (1995) is a popular military-psychology book whose empirical basis has been criticised; it is cited as the source of the hypothesis, not as evidence for it.
5. "Small canines, flat nails, no armour, thin skin" in §4 is textbook comparative anatomy and carries no specific citation.
6. New term **zero range** (§2) to be added to `static/data/ko.json` as `proposed`, with the §2 definition as its verbatim defining sentence.
