## §0 Sammanfattning

*Ögat ser för att det delar.*

Vision and division do not share a root: *videre* "to see" descends from PIE \*weid-, while the *-videre* in *dividere* descends from \*(d)uid- "to separate" **[E]**. The two roots sit one sound apart, and the post treats that near-homophony as a pointer only **[M]**. The structural claim is separate: depth in vision, the sacred, the individual and every working gate are each produced by a maintained difference rather than by a unity, and in each case the difference carries information instead of blocking it **[D]**. This inverts the reading of [The Cloven Section](/posts/the-cloven-section/) (DRK-174), where a cut that does not split is an obstruction. The post does not claim that every division is productive, and it does not claim the etymologies prove the structure.

## §1 Two roots, one breath apart

### §1.1 The seeing root [E]

Latin *videre* "to see" goes back to PIE \*weid- "to see", which gives *vision* and, by a 1935 coinage modelled on *audio*, *video*, the first person singular "I see" (Etymonline, *vision*, *video*) **[E]**. The same root gives English *wit*, Old Norse *vita* and Swedish *vett*, so that to know is, in Germanic, to have seen (Etymonline, *wit*) **[E]**. Greek *idein* "to see" and *idea* belong to it as well (Etymonline, *idea*), and *eidōlon*, image or apparition, comes from *eidos*, form (Etymonline, *idol*) **[E]**.

### §1.2 The separating root [E]

Latin *dividere* is *dis-* "apart" plus *-videre*, which de Vaan derives from PIE \*(d)uid- "to separate, distinguish", the initial *d* lost by dissimilation (Etymonline, *divide*) **[E]**. *Widow* goes back to \*uidh- "to separate, divide", with Latin *vidua*, *viduus* "bereft" and Sanskrit *vidhavā* "widow" as cognates (Etymonline, *widow*) **[E]**. Whether \*(d)uid- and \*uidh- are one root or two is not settled in the sources consulted here **[H]**.

### §1.3 What the near-homophony is worth [M]

*Vision* and *division* differ by one consonant cluster in PIE and by two letters in English, and Latin itself wrote both with *-videre* **[M]**. The resemblance is a pointer, and nothing below rests on it: the argument of §2–§6 holds for any language in which seeing and separating are unrelated words **[M]**.

## §2 The eye divides

### §2.1 Depth from disparity [E]

Wheatstone (1838) showed with the stereoscope that two flat drawings, each the view one eye would receive, fuse into a single solid when presented one to each eye. Depth appears only when the two images differ, and identical images give a flat picture **[E]**. Binocular depth is therefore computed from the disparity between two views, not from either view alone (Wheatstone 1838) **[E]**.

### §2.2 Formal sketch (analogy, not derivation)

Let the visual field be covered by two open sets $U_L, U_R$, the regions seen by the left and right eye, with overlap $U_{LR} = U_L \cap U_R$. Each eye supplies a section $s_L$ on $U_L$ and $s_R$ on $U_R$, valued in image intensities over $\mathbb R$. In ordinary gluing the Čech difference

$$\delta(s_L, s_R) = s_R|_{U_{LR}} - s_L|_{U_{LR}}$$

must vanish for a global section to exist, and a nonzero value is an obstruction. Stereopsis does the reverse: the visual system treats $\delta$ as data, converting it into a depth map $d: U_{LR} \to \mathbb R_{>0}$, so that the global object is built from the difference rather than despite it **[D]**. In [The Cloven Section](/posts/the-cloven-section/) (DRK-174) a cut whose difference cannot be lifted is mere severance. In the eye the difference is the content **[D]**.

### §2.3 The watch, not the organ [D]

[Övervakaren och Undervakaren](/posts/overvakaren-och-undervakaren/) (DRK-178) argued that what is inherited is the watch, not the eye **[D]**. §2.2 adds a condition: a watch that sees depth must hold two positions apart and read their disagreement. A single viewpoint, however powerful, sees no depth, which is the optical form of the anti-totalisation principle **[D]**.

## §3 The divider that lets through

### §3.1 Wall, door, port, window [E]

English *wall* is borrowed from Latin *vallum*, a rampart topped by a palisade, perhaps from *vallus* "stake" (Etymonline, *wall*) **[E]**. Latin *porta* "gate" and *portus* "harbour", originally "entrance, passage", both go back to PIE \*per- "to lead, pass over" (Etymonline, *port*) **[E]**. A port is named for the crossing, not for the barrier **[E]**. English *window* is Old Norse *vindauga*, "wind-eye" (Etymonline, *window*) **[E]**. The opening in the divider is called an eye, so the vocabulary closes the loop between §2 and §3 **[M]**.

### §3.2 Membrane [E]

*Membrane* is Latin *membrana*, the skin that covers the *membra*, the limbs (Etymonline, *membrane*) **[E]**. A cell membrane is selectively permeable: its lipid bilayer blocks most solutes, while channels and transporters let specific ones across (Alberts et al. 2014) **[E]**. The membrane is the case where division and passage are one structure **[E]**. A cell with no membrane has no inside, and a cell with no channels is dead **[S]**.

### §3.3 Keeper, fence, offence [D]

[The Keeper at the Crossing](/posts/the-keeper-at-the-crossing/) (DRK-183) traced offence and defence to the same Latin *fendere*, with the fence deciding which way the strike points **[D]**. [The Generalizard](/posts/the-generalizard/) (DRK-150) defined the keeper as a node whose restriction maps are gated against the diffusion gradient **[D]**. In this post's terms, a keeper is a divider with a port, and the port is where the keeper's judgement sits **[D]**. A wall without a gate is a prison or a tomb, a gate without a wall is a ceremony, and only the pair keeps anything **[D]**.

## §4 The cut precinct

### §4.1 Temenos and templum

Greek *temenos*, the sacred ground around a temple, is literally "the place cut off", from *temnein* "to cut" (Etymonline, *temple*) **[E]**. Latin *templum*, first the consecrated ground for taking auspices, is derived by Watkins from \*tem- "to cut" and by Klein and de Vaan from \*temp- "to stretch" (Etymonline, *temple*) **[H]**. *Atom* comes from the same *temnein*: *a-tomos*, the uncut (Etymonline, *atom*) **[E]**.

### §4.2 The sacred as set apart [E]

Durkheim (1912) defined sacred things as things set apart and surrounded by prohibitions, and religion as the system of beliefs and practices relative to them **[E]**. On that definition the sacred is constituted by a maintained division from the profane, and it lapses when the division is no longer kept (Durkheim 1912) **[S]**. *Divine* is from Latin *divus*, not from *dividere*, so the divine–divide pairing is sound only **[M]**. The structural pairing does not need it: the temenos shows the sacred made by cutting **[D]**.

### §4.3 Anubis at the border of life [D]

Anubis, Inpu, is the Egyptian god of funerary practice, shown weighing the heart in the Book of the Dead (Britannica, *Anubis*) **[E]**. [Inpu Means Input](/posts/inpu-means-input/) (DRK-155) read him as the restriction map at the boundary of the life-manifold, an operator who weighs against a standard he is forbidden to author **[D]**. In this post's terms Anubis is a port-keeper on the divide between life and death: he does not rule either side, he keeps the crossing **[D]**.

### §4.4 The Mandate of Heaven [D]

The Zhou used the Mandate of Heaven to justify overthrowing the Shang: Heaven grants rule to the virtuous and withdraws it from the unjust, and Mencius later used the doctrine to justify rebellion (Wikipedia, *Mandate of Heaven*) **[E]**. [The Generalizard](/posts/the-generalizard/) (DRK-150) read the mandate as the keeper-function at civilisational scale, and [The Mandate of Reality](/posts/the-mandate-of-reality/) (DRK-151) as the right to define what is real **[D]**. The mandate is a revocable division between legitimate and illegitimate rule, a gate that can close behind the ruler who passed through it **[D]**.

## §5 In-dividuus

### §5.1 Cicero's atoms and images [E]

In *De finibus* I.17 Cicero renders Democritus' *atomos* as *corpora individua propter soliditatem*, bodies undivided because solid (Cicero, *De finibus* I.17) **[E]**. Latin *individuus*, "not divisible", is *in-* plus *dividuus*, and its neuter *individuum* meant an atom (Etymonline, *individual*) **[E]**. Four sections later, Cicero reports the atomist theory of sight: *imagines*, which the Greeks call *eidola*, strike us so that we see and also think (Cicero, *De finibus* I.21) **[E]**. One paragraph of Latin holds the undivided body and the seeing root (\*weid-, through *eidōlon*) side by side **[D]**.

### §5.2 Indivisibility is scale-relative [D]

The *atomos* was split in the twentieth century, and each later "uncuttable" has been a claim about the reach of the knife, not about matter **[D]**. A final indivisible unit is a totalising claim in the sense of the anti-totalisation principle, and the corpus therefore treats every individual as undivided at a stated scale only **[D]**.

### §5.3 Two ways to the single person [S]

Finnish *yksilö*, individual, is built from *yksi* "one", so Finnish reaches the person through oneness where Latin reaches it through non-division **[S]**. Strathern (1988) described Melanesian persons as partible composites of relations, the dividual, against which the Western individual is one cultural limit, not a universal **[S]**. The Swedish *individ* borrows the Latin route **[S]**.

## §6 Divide and rule, oppression, the bite across the line

### §6.1 Rule by blocking the glue [D]

*Divide et impera* names rule by keeping the governed from gluing into one section. Its classical origin is not attested in the sources consulted here, so the maxim is used only as a name **[D]**. In sheaf terms the ruler manufactures obstructions, so that local resistances never extend to a global one **[D]**. [The Totalitarian Sheaf](/posts/the-totalitarian-sheaf/) (DRK-125) described the opposite limit, where all restriction maps are forced open; the two are the closed-gate and no-gate failures of §3.3 **[D]**.

### §6.2 Oppression as pressure on the divider [E]

*Oppress* is Latin *opprimere*, *ob-* "against" plus *premere* "to press", first physical and later figurative (Etymonline, *oppress*) **[E]**. Oppression is pressure held against a dividing line from one side, so that the line moves only one way **[D]**. The reading in [The Compartmentalized Manifold](/posts/the-compartmentalized-manifold/) (DRK-161) of engineered obstruction applies: the difference is kept for extraction, not for information **[D]**.

### §6.3 The bite across the border [D]

[The Bite](/posts/the-bite/) (DRK-184) showed the varanid clinch as mutual hostage: each body lies inside the other's bite and each withholds **[D]**. [The Boundary of Us](/posts/the-boundary-of-us/) (DRK-124) argued that a boundary without such a reality-test collapses into closure **[D]**. The bite across the line is the counter to §6.1: a division that both sides can reach through stays honest, while one that only one side can cross becomes oppression **[D]**.

## §7 Vidare

### §7.1 What has gone apart [S]

Swedish *vid*, wide, is Old Swedish *vīþer*, Old Norse *víðr*, from Germanic \*wida-, which Hellquist (1922) relates to PIE \*wi- "apart" and, following Prellwitz, glosses as "that which has gone apart" **[S]**. *Vidare*, further, is its comparative: the further is what has gone more apart **[S]**. Whether \*wi- "apart" connects to \*(d)uid- "separate" of §1.2 is not shown in the sources consulted **[H]**.

### §7.2 The pejorative vid [S]

Hellquist (1922) analyses *vidskepelse*, superstition, as *skipilse*, condition, with *vid* in a pejorative sense comparable to *van-* in *vantro* **[S]**. *Vidunder*, monster, is formed the same way as a pejorative or prodigious *under*, wonder (Hellquist 1922) **[S]**. Swedish thus files false belief and the monster under the same off-side prefix, which [Roots](/posts/roots/) (DRK-200) could test across languages as a candidate hyperliminal joint **[H]**.

### §7.3 Video [M]

*Video* means "I see", and the screen that carries it divides the seer from the seen **[M]**. Read with §2, a screen with one image gives the flat picture of an identical-image stereoscope, and depth would require a second, disagreeing view **[M]**.

## §8 Falsification (DRK-131)

**F1: Root identity.** §1 claims *dividere* and *videre* descend from different PIE roots, and is **Refuted** if de Vaan's Latin etymological dictionary derives the *-videre* of *dividere* from \*weid- "to see".

**F2: Depth from difference.** §2 reads binocular disparity as the source of near depth, and is **Refuted** if measured stereoacuity at near range is no better with two eyes than with one.

**F3: Scope limit.** If F1–F2 fail, the etymologies of §3, §4.1, §5.1 and §7 stand on their own sources, while the structural readings tagged **[D]** in §2, §3.3, §4 and §6 lose their anchor.
