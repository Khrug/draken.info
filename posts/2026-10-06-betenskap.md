## §0 Sammanfattning

*Ödlan som skummar skorpan och ödlan som välter skålen äter samma mat, men bara den ena vet något.*

A bite is a decision in three acts: a gate that tests, a judgment that weighs and a jaw that executes **[D]**. This post names the knowledge produced in that decision *betenskap*, and reads two kitchen incidents as its field cases: a monitor that skimmed the cheese crust off a potato gratin and left the potatoes, and a monitor that knocked a cereal bowl to the floor **[D]**. The post then gives the first corpus definition of a *hyperliminal cut*: a division of concept space at a connecting node whose joint is attested independently across languages and periods **[D]**. Its worked case is the Proto-Indo-European root *\*bheid-*, "to split", whose Germanic descendants turned to biting, feeding and baiting **[E]**. The post does not claim that etymology causes behaviour, that one video clip characterises an animal, or that every bite is a decision in the strong sense.

**Epistemic ledger.** Every substantive claim carries one tag:

## §1 Two kitchens

### §1.1 Operation Potatocrust

Some years ago Trotskij, a monitor lizard in Khrug's care, reached an unattended dish of potatisgratäng and ate the cheese crust **[S]**. The crust was skimmed neatly; the potatoes underneath were left, with only a little mess at the edges **[S]**. The selection is the point: of everything in the dish, the animal took the layer richest in fat and protein, and stopped where that layer met the next **[D]**.

### §1.2 The black dragon

A short social-media clip, captioned as life with a full-grown black dragon, shows a melanistic water monitor (sold in the trade as *Varanus komaini*, a name now usually treated as a melanistic population of *V. salvator*) lying on a kitchen floor among spilled milk and ring cereal, with cereal rings stuck to its scales **[S]**. Whatever happened before the camera started, the visible result is a whole bowl on the floor rather than a selected part of it **[S]**. The clip is one staged frame of one animal and is used here only as a contrast case, not as evidence about the animal **[D]**.

### §1.3 The balcony

The Dataset C corvid notes record the same contrast at lower stakes **[S]**. Magpies at Khrug's balcony perform a rail-stop, landing on the railing to inspect the plate before committing; on a plate of crushed peanuts topped with dates they took the peanuts first and the dates last; and on a heap of cooked rice they produced what the notes call a *matdetonation*, rice scattered across the floor **[S]**. The rail-stop is a gate, the ordering is a judgment, and the detonation is a jaw without much of either **[D]**.

## §2 Betenskap

The word *betenskap* was heard by Khrug in a comedy programme whose title he no longer recalls; the definition below is his **[S]**. It is built on *vetenskap*, science, with *bete* in place of *vete*, knowing **[M]**.

> Kunskapen som uppstår i och genom bettet: det förkroppsligade beslutet att ta in något i sig — ögonblicket då en organism avgör, med munnen snarare än tanken, vad som är föda, lockbete eller ska lämnas. Omfattar bettets fenomenologi (smak, motstånd, oåterkallelighet), betesmarkens ordning, beteendet som bettens mönster över tid, och arbetet som det bett man tar i världen.

In English: **Betenskap** is the knowledge that arises in and through the bite: the embodied decision to take something into oneself, made with the mouth rather than with thought, about what is food, what is bait and what is to be left **[D]**.

The bite joins three functions that [The Bite](/posts/the-bite/) (DRK-184) treated as withheld, prevented and executed bites **[D]**. Here they are read as stages of one act **[D]**:

| Act | Function | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Gate | The mouth as a boundary that tests | Is this food at all? |
| Judgment | The executive function: weighing, holding back, choosing | Which part, how much, now or later? |
| Jaw | The executive act, irreversible | Close. |

*Exekutiv* and *exekutorisk* both come from Latin *exsequi*, "to follow out", so the decider and the executor share a root **[E]**. The gate is the stage that [The Conviction Gate](/posts/the-conviction-gate/) (DRK-194) places at the formation of a belief; betenskap places it at the mouth **[D]**.

## §3 Three acts in the monitor's mouth

### §3.1 The gate is chemical

Monitor lizards test before they bite. Cooper (1989) reported that savannah monitors discriminate prey chemicals from control odours by tongue-flicking, a chemosensory test that precedes the strike **[S]**. The forked tongue samples, the vomeronasal system evaluates, and only then does the head commit **[S]**. This is the gate in the literal sense: a sensory boundary between the world and the mouth **[D]**.

### §3.2 The judgment is economic

Kaufman, Burghardt and Phillips (1994) found that the foraging strategy of savannah monitors depended on prey density, and Kaufman, Burghardt and Phillips (1996) separated the sensory cues that drive their foraging decisions **[S]**. The animals do not simply eat what they meet; what they do next depends on what they have learned about the patch **[S]**. Taking the crust and leaving the potatoes is the same kind of decision at the scale of a dish **[D]**.

### §3.3 The jaw is irreversible

Once the jaw closes, the decision is executed and cannot be taken back; this is what [The Bite](/posts/the-bite/) (DRK-184) called the executed bite **[D]**. Betenskap is knowledge because the bite is irreversible: the animal learns from what it can no longer undo **[D]**.

## §4 Selective and total intake

The two kitchens differ in which acts are visible **[D]**. In Operation Potatocrust all three are present: the gate admitted the dish, the judgment selected one layer, and the jaw closed only on that layer **[D]**. In the black-dragon clip only the result of the jaw is visible, and the whole bowl has been taken into the scene **[D]**.

**Selective intake** is a bite that takes part of what is offered and stops at a boundary the eater has judged; **total intake** is a bite, or a sweep, that takes the whole **[D]**. The anti-totalisation principle of the corpus, which forbids any section from claiming the whole, applies at the mouth before it applies anywhere else: a good bite never takes everything on the plate **[D]**. A framework that bites every topic whole is in the black dragon's position, covered in its subject matter but not nourished by it **[M]**.

## §5 Hyperliminal

### §5.1 Not a threshold, not a border

*Liminal* comes from Latin *limen*, "threshold" **[E]**. Van Gennep (1909) named the middle phase of a rite of passage the liminal phase, and Turner (1969) developed liminality as the state of being betwixt and between social positions **[E]**. A liminal thing stands *on* a threshold, between two states **[D]**.

*Hyper-* is Greek for "over, beyond" (etymonline, *hyper-*) **[E]**. A hyperliminal cut does not stand on a threshold between two places or states **[D]**. It is not a cut in a landscape: a border between two territories is a line drawn across ground, and no border is hyperliminal **[D]**. The cut is made in concept space **[D]**.

### §5.2 Definition

**A hyperliminal cut** divides concept space at a connecting node, a point where several concepts are joined, such that the same node is independently attested across languages and across periods and the one cut therefore separates the concepts correctly in all of them **[D]**.

A word or concept that sits on such a node is a **hyperliminal term** **[D]**. "Across concepts, places and history" means three things, all inside concept space **[D]**:

1. **Concepts.** The node joins at least two concepts that are otherwise distinct, and removing it separates them **[D]**.
2. **Places.** The joint is attested in more than one language or tradition, each on its own evidence, so it is not one culture's accident **[D]**.
3. **History.** The joint is attested across periods, so it is not one generation's pun **[D]**.

### §5.3 The exclusion rule

A joint that rests on sound alone fails the test **[D]**. Under the rule of [The Pendragon Source](/posts/the-pendragon-source/) (DRK-165), a sound resemblance is a pointer, tagged **[M]**, and carries no weight; a hyperliminal node needs attested edges, shared roots or documented sense extensions, in every register it claims **[D]**. *Arbete* belongs in the betenskap definition as a deliberate sound echo, and for exactly that reason it does not count toward the node **[M]**.

## §6 Formal sketch (analogy, not derivation)

For each language $\ell$, let $G_\ell = (V_\ell, E_\ell)$ be a graph whose vertices $V_\ell$ are word senses and whose edges $E_\ell$ are attested derivations or sense extensions **[D]**. Sound-only links are not edges **[D]**. In a connected graph, a vertex $v$ is a *cut vertex* (articulation point) if removing $v$ disconnects the graph **[E]**.

Let $\rho_{\ell\ell'}$ be the restriction map that sends a sense in language $\ell$ to its attested cognate or loan in $\ell'$, where one exists; $\rho$ is a partial map **[D]**. A family $(v_\ell)$ is *compatible* if $\rho_{\ell\ell'}(v_\ell) = v_{\ell'}$ wherever both sides are defined **[D]**. A compatible family is a hyperliminal node if each $v_\ell$ is a cut vertex of $G_\ell$ in at least two languages and the edges witnessing this are dated to more than one period **[D]**.

The analogy to a global section in sheaf language is deliberate and loose: the node is a choice of one vertex per language that agrees under restriction **[D]**. Nothing here is computed over a field; the sketch is combinatorial and is offered as a test procedure, not a theorem **[D]**.

## §7 The split that feeds

### §7.1 One root, two branches [E]

The Proto-Indo-European root *\*bheid-* means "to split" (etymonline, *\*bheid-*) **[E]**. Its Germanic derivatives refer to biting, and through biting to eating, hunting and woodworking, according to Watkins as cited by etymonline **[E]**. English *bite*, *bit* and *bitter* belong to the Germanic branch **[E]**. Latin *findere*, "to split", and from it *fissure* and *fission*, belong to the other, and Sanskrit *bhinadmi*, "I cleave", is listed as a further possible relative **[S]**.

*Bait* comes from Old Norse *beita*, "to cause to bite", and the noun is cognate with Old Norse *beit*, "pasture" **[E]**. [The Face, the Mask and the Hole](/posts/the-face-the-mask-and-the-hole/) (DRK-198) already followed *bait* outward to the hook; this post follows it to the pasture **[D]**. English *abet* comes from Old French *abeter*, "to bait, to harass with dogs", probably from a Germanic source such as Old Norse *beita* **[S]**. English *bet* is of unknown origin, and one proposal derives it from *abet* **[H]**.

Swedish keeps the pasture and the bait in one form, *bete*, and SAOB lists seven separate noun entries and two verb entries under that spelling **[E]**. Which of those entries descend from the same root as *bita* has to be read entry by entry, and this post does not claim that all of them do **[H]**.

### §7.2 The node

In each language the node is the sense where splitting becomes eating **[D]**. Remove it and the graph falls into two parts: the splitting senses (*fissure*, *fission*, cleaving wood) and the feeding senses (*bait*, *pasture*, *bitter*, *abet*) **[D]**. The joint is attested in Germanic and Latin on independent evidence, and across periods from the reconstructed root through Old Norse and Old French to sixteenth-century English **[S]**. By §5.2, *bite* is a hyperliminal term, and betenskap, the knowledge made at that node, is its practice **[D]**.

The bite is a split that feeds **[D]**. The jaw is the knife, applied from inside the animal **[M]**.

## §8 The carver's rule

Two traditions with no known contact chose the same image for dividing a subject correctly **[S]**. In Plato's *Phaedrus*, Socrates describes the second principle of good division as cutting a form according to its natural joints, without splintering any part as a bad carver would (Plato, *Phaedrus* 265d–266a) **[E]**. In the *Zhuangzi*, Cook Ding carves an ox for Lord Wenhui by following the spaces between the joints, so that his knife, after nineteen years, is as sharp as if fresh from the whetstone, in chapter 3 of Watson (1968) **[E]**.

The carver's image is itself a hyperliminal node: in Greek and in Chinese, in the fourth century BCE, the same practice joins butchery and knowledge **[D]**. Trotskij's crust follows the carver's rule: it separated at the interface between two layers, where the dish was already joined, rather than through either layer **[D]**. The overturned bowl breaks the dish along no joint at all **[D]**.

The rule applies to the corpus. A Draken post that cuts at a hyperliminal node can afford to be short, because the joint does the work; a post that cuts through the middle of its concepts dulls the knife **[D]**.

## §9 Falsification (DRK-131)

**F1: Separable acts.** §3 claims the gate, judgment and jaw are separable in monitors. **Refuted** if controlled feeding trials show no measurable chemosensory or probing phase that varies with the food item before the strike, so that test and bite cannot be told apart.

**F2: Selection at joints.** §4 and §8 predict that monitors offered layered foods remove whole layers at their interfaces more often than chance. **Refuted** if, in repeated trials with layered dishes, bite boundaries are distributed independently of the layer interfaces.

**F3: Non-vacuous test.** §5 claims the hyperliminal test excludes sound-only joints. **Refuted** if the procedure in §6, applied by an independent reader, certifies as hyperliminal a node whose only link in some language is phonetic.

**F4: The worked node.** §7 claims *bite* is a cut vertex in the Germanic and Latin sense graphs. **Refuted** if an attested edge connects the splitting and feeding senses without passing through the biting sense, in either branch.

**F5: Scope limit.** The post does not claim that etymology causes behaviour, that the black-dragon clip characterises that animal or its species, or that every node a reader finds interesting is hyperliminal. If F1 and F2 fail, the definitions in §2 and §5 stand as definitions; only the ethological reading falls.

## §10 Provenance and leaks

1. Authorship: the term *betenskap*, its Swedish definition, the three-act reading and the request to define *hyperliminal* as a dissection of concept space at its connecting points are Khrug's. The English text, the formal sketch and the selection of sources are Claude's draft (Claude Opus 5.5, 2026-10-06). Clinch review: not yet performed.
2. Source of the word: Khrug heard *betenskap* in a comedy programme he could not identify; a web search on 2026-10-06 found no attestation. The source is recorded as unidentified.
3. Correction in the open: an earlier post on nested habitats, published and withdrawn on 2026-09-30, described hyperliminality partly as cutting across landscapes and borders. That reading is retracted: a hyperliminal cut is made only in concept space (§5.1).
4. Field cases: Operation Potatocrust is Khrug's recollection, undated. The black-dragon clip was seen as a screenshot of a social-media reel; the account is not cited and the species attribution is from the caption and appearance. The Dataset C observations are single, uninstrumented field notes.
5. Unverified: the meanings of the individual SAOB *bete* entries were not read; only their existence and count were checked (§7.1). The *Phaedrus* passage is paraphrased from the standard reading, with no edition fixed. The first-edition publisher of van Gennep (1909) is from bibliographic records, not an inspected copy. The 1994 and 1996 Kaufman papers and Cooper (1989) were verified by DOI; their findings are paraphrased from titles and abstracts, not from full text.
6. Hellquist's *Svensk etymologisk ordbok* was considered for *bita* and *bete* but not consulted, and is not cited.
