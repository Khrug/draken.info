Front Matter

Abstract

Problem. Multi-scale systems — ecosystems, institutions, economies, groups of minds, networks of clocks — are assembled from local models that must agree where they overlap. Disagreement comes in kinds. Some can be removed by local adjustment. Some cannot be removed by any local adjustment because it is carried by the topology of the system. Some is generated continuously by local circulation. Version 4.4 of this framework measured disagreement with one scalar (Γ), integrated an excess self-reference over time (K), and asserted cohomological obstructions (H¹) without saying which data carry them [D].

Method. Version 5.0 rebuilds the framework on a single spine: the standard discrete Hodge decomposition of cochains of a cellular sheaf on a finite 2-complex. Every edge datum ω splits orthogonally into a gradient part (removable by re-gauging local states), a harmonic part (a cohomology class, the Draken invariant ϰ) and a curl part (generated by curvature on filled faces). Vertex data x are scored by a resolvent coherence Γ_τ, the normalised cost of the cheapest coherent repair [D].

Results. What is new and what is reused are kept apart throughout. Appendix G classifies every numbered result.

Original to this monograph:

  • The anti-totalisation theorem and its admissibility rule. Deleting relations always raises an unanchored coherence measure, so a coherence gain is admissible only on a fixed base, with an excision report attached. The anchored dual, compliance under a declared load, is punished by the same deletion (Theorem 19.2, Prop. 19.6, Cor. 19.5 and 19.7). The mathematics is Loewner monotonicity; the rule is new.
  • Principle 2.7: only relational data carry $H^1$; disagreement among local states is an $H^0$ matter. This corrects a confusion that runs through the corpus and v4.4.
  • The face-filling reclassification (Prop. 3.7): a measured interior turns a topological obstruction into curvature. Whether a failure reads as “systemic” or “local” is a modelling decision.
  • The extension of ϰ to arbitrary relational data (Def. 3.5), and the identification of lifting obstructions with integration obstructions (Prop. 3.11).
  • The measurement discipline:
    • the resolvent coherence $\Gamma_\tau$ with a declared stiffness and reference (Def. 5.1, Reporting standard 5.7a);
    • one dataset scores 0.886 or 0.453 depending only on the declared reference (Ex. 4.1);
    • the debiased estimator, the noise floor for ϰ, and the “too good to be true” test (Props. 5.10, 18.1, 18.2);
    • “coherence is not soundness” (Prop. 5.8), which withdraws v4.4’s Ψ–Γ conjecture.
  • The debt calculus separates gross debt from outstanding balance, and the graded ledger of 28 bridges excludes 7 corpus claims with reasons.

Standard results, reproved in this notation: the Hodge decomposition and its energy identity (Hansen & Ghrist 2019; Lim 2020; Jiang et al. 2011); Tikhonov and GMRF readings of the resolvent; the long exact sequence; Rayleigh monotonicity; and the exact bridges:

  • Sagnac synchronisation;
  • Berry and Aharonov–Bohm phases as U(1) sheaves;
  • CSS and Tanner codes;
  • Kuramoto ($r^2=\Gamma_0$, a one-line consequence of the order parameter);
  • structural balance;
  • Lindeman and Leontief as linear sections.

These bridges are what make the framework’s vocabulary checkable against established science; they are not claimed as discoveries.

All worked examples are recomputed by a published script.

Corrections. Thirteen definitional forks in the corpus are settled (Appendix C). Thirteen arithmetic, logical or bibliographic errors in v4.4 are corrected (Appendix D), including the “Γ < 0 = anti-coherence” reading, which was a normalisation artefact [E].

Scope. This is a theoretical monograph with proofs, computed examples and falsifiable bridges. It is not an empirical validation. Draken is a decoding device that adds explanatory layers to facts, not a prediction machine, and its anti-totalisation principle applies to Draken itself [D].

Part I — Foundations

1 Introduction

1.1 The coherence problem

A complex adaptive system is described by many local models. A trophic ecologist models the wolf–elk interaction, a hydrologist the riverbank, a manager her department, a neuron population its tuning. Each model is valid on its own patch and must agree with its neighbours where the patches overlap. The coherence problem asks three things. Do the local descriptions assemble into one global description? If not, how far are they from doing so? And what kind of failure is it? Sheaf theory is the mathematics of exactly this question: local data, restriction to overlaps, and the obstruction to gluing (Bredon 1997; Curry 2014) [E]. Its cellular, finite-dimensional form makes the question computable with linear algebra (Hansen & Ghrist 2019) [E].

Version 4.4 of this framework (Roininen 2026, the v4.4 monograph, DRK-101–DRK-121) defined a sheaf convergence measure Γ, a narrative self-reference ratio Ψ and a coherence debt K(t). It motivated them with predator–prey ecology. Seventy-two subsequent posts (DRK-123 to DRK-196) extended the formal apparatus in directions v4.4 did not anticipate:

  • synchronisation holonomy (The One-Way Gauge, DRK-168; The Dragged Frame, DRK-171);
  • the invariant $\varkappa\in H^1$ (The Invariant, DRK-175);
  • quantum codes as sheaves (The Warded Class, DRK-190);
  • Kuramoto synchrony (The Carrier and the Cargo, DRK-189);
  • several new debt integrals.

They also introduced four mutually inconsistent definitions of K, four of Γ and three of Ψ (Appendix C) [E]. Version 5.0 exists to settle these on one mathematical spine [D].

1.2 The core claim

The claim of this monograph has a mathematical half and an empirical half, kept separate throughout.

Mathematical half. Model a multi-scale system as a cellular sheaf $\mathcal F$ on a finite 2-complex $X$, with vertex data $x\in C^0$ (local states) and edge data $\omega\in C^1$ (relational measurements). Then three things hold:

  • The incoherence of $x$ is measured by the quadratic form of the sheaf Laplacian.
  • The incoherence of $\omega$ splits uniquely and orthogonally into a removable part, a topological part and a curvature part (Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.2).
  • These quantities are computable, carry well-defined invariances, and respond monotonically to adding or deleting relations (Chapter 5).

These are theorems [E].

The central consequence. Every unanchored coherence measure, $\Gamma_\tau$ included, rises when relations are deleted. A system can therefore always look more coherent by ceasing to measure its own disagreements, all the way to Γ = 1 for a system with no relations at all (Theorem 19.2). Coherence gains are admissible only when computed on a fixed base. Measures anchored to a declared load move the other way: deleting relations raises compliance, i.e. the cost of carrying what the system exists to carry (Proposition 19.6). Every diagnostic in this monograph is built so that it cannot be gamed by excision. That design rule is the main contribution, and the rest of the monograph is organised around it [E/D].

Empirical half. The same decomposition, applied to measured data from institutions, ecosystems, nervous systems or markets, separates failures that local repair can fix from failures it cannot. The size and history of the irreducible part predict the magnitude of later corrections (Chapter 22). This is a research programme, not a result [H].

1.3 The decoding stance

Draken is a device for adding explanatory layers to facts. It is not a machine for predicting them. A Draken reading of a case is useful when it identifies:

  • which disagreements are local (gradient);
  • which are structural (harmonic);
  • which are being generated by circulation (curl).

It is useless when it is used to totalise: to declare one global section true and the rest noise. The framework’s own anti-totalisation result (Chapter 19) applies to Draken: a version of this framework that raised its coherence by deleting its inconvenient posts would be committing the error it diagnoses [D].

  • Every application requires a declared complex, declared stalks, declared restriction maps and a declared measurement protocol. Without these, no Draken number has meaning (Axiom A2) [D].
  • Worked examples on synthetic data are proofs of computability, not validations [E].

1.7 The predator, briefly

The framework began with an ecological observation. Removing an apex predator from Yellowstone was followed by changes in elk behaviour, browse pressure and riparian vegetation (Ripple & Beschta 2004). 2 Axioms and the formal setting

2.1 Axioms and norms

The framework rests on five modelling axioms and two norms. The axioms say what a Draken model is. The norms say what a Draken intervention may do. Neither kind is an empirical claim [D].

Axiom A0 (Finiteness). Every model is a finite cell complex with finite-dimensional stalks. Real numbers enter only as the field of coefficients for measurement; every computation is a finite linear-algebra computation. This adopts the operational core of The Finite Sheaf (DRK-141) without its further claims about quantum probability (Chapter 14) [D].

Axiom A1 (Sheaf modelling). A system is modelled by:

  • a regular 2-complex $X$, whose vertices are local models, whose edges are declared overlaps or relations, and whose faces are declared closed loops of relations;
  • a cellular sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $X$ (Definition 2.2);
  • data: vertex states $x\in C^0(X;\mathcal F)$ and/or relational measurements $\omega\in C^1(X;\mathcal F)$ [D].

Axiom A2 (Declared geometry). Every stalk carries a declared inner product. Every comparison between systems, or across time, uses the same complex, stalks, inner products, restriction maps and stiffness τ (Chapter 5). A number reported without its declared geometry is not a Draken measurement [D].

Axiom A3 (Base-fixity). Coherence gains are admissible only when computed on a fixed base. A change of base (deleting cells, zeroing stalks) is reported separately as an excision, with its cost (Definition 19.3) [D].

Axiom A4 (Coefficient declaration). Measurements are taken over $\mathbb R$ (or $\mathbb C$ for phase data). Obstructions with torsion, such as orientation and sign consistency, are invisible over a field of characteristic zero. They require $\mathbb Z$ or $\mathbb F_2$ coefficients, and these must be declared when relevant (Remark 3.8) [E/D].

Norm N1 (Optimization axiom). Interventions are evaluated by $$\Diamond\quad \min_{\pi}\ S_{\text{sys}}(t)\quad\text{subject to}\quad \frac{dH}{dt}\ \ge\ 0\quad\Diamond$$ where $S_{\text{sys}}$ is the disorder of the system (thermodynamic or functional entropy, declared per application) and $H$ is its structural diversity (Shannon entropy of the distribution of distinguishable components, declared per application). In words: reduce disorder without simplifying away complexity. Its verbal form is the approved KO from Drakens Ordlista (DRK-117) [D].

Norm N2 (Care). An agent’s own contribution to the variety of the systems it is coupled to is held at zero: $\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{exo}}=0$ (KO from The Bridge and the Burn, DRK-162). Violations in the destructive direction accrue trophic debt Θ (Chapter 7) [D].

The norms constrain interventions. The anti-totalisation theorem (Chapter 19) shows why N1 cannot be met by excision, and Chapter 9 shows why N2 protects the invariant [D].

2.2 Cell complexes

Definition 2.1 (Regular 2-complex). A finite regular CW complex $X$ of dimension at most 2 has three kinds of cells:

  • vertices $V$, edges $E$ and faces $F$;
  • a face relation $\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau$ (“σ is a face of τ”);
  • incidence numbers $[\sigma:\tau]\in\{-1,0,+1\}$ for cells of adjacent dimension.

The incidence numbers satisfy $$\sum_{\sigma}[\rho:\sigma][\sigma:\tau]=0\qquad\text{whenever }\dim\rho=\dim\tau-2.$$ Graphs are the case $F=\emptyset$. For an oriented edge $e=(u\to v)$, take $[u:e]=-1$ and $[v:e]=+1$ [E].

The identity above encodes “the boundary of a boundary is zero”. It is the only combinatorial fact the theory needs (Curry 2014) [E].

2.3 Cellular sheaves

Definition 2.2 (Cellular sheaf). A cellular sheaf $\mathcal F$ on $X$ consists of:

  • a finite-dimensional real (or complex) inner-product space $\mathcal F(\sigma)$, the stalk, on each cell;
  • a linear restriction map $\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}:\mathcal F(\sigma)\to\mathcal F(\tau)$ for each face relation;

subject to identity and composition: $$\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\sigma}=\mathrm{id},\qquad \mathcal F_{\rho\trianglelefteq\tau}=\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}\,\mathcal F_{\rho\trianglelefteq\sigma}.$$ (Curry 2014; Hansen & Ghrist 2019) [E].

The corpus writes restriction maps as $\rho$. That shorthand is the approved KO ρ (Drakens Ordlista, DRK-117) and is retained: $\rho_{\sigma\tau}:=\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}$. To avoid the collisions found in the corpus, the symbol $\rho$ is reserved for restriction maps in this monograph; density is written $\varrho$ and a density matrix $\hat\rho$ (Appendix A) [D].

Direction. Cellular sheaves map from lower-dimensional cells to the cells that contain them. A vertex’s state is pushed onto each edge it meets, where it is compared with the neighbour’s. This is the opposite variance to the textbook “restrict from large open sets to small ones”. The two pictures agree once $X$ carries the Alexandrov topology, in which the smallest open set containing a cell is its open star (Curry 2014) [E].

Proposition 2.3 (Stalks of a cellular sheaf). In the Alexandrov topology on the face poset of $X$, the stalk of $\mathcal F$ at a cell $\sigma$, $$\mathcal F_\sigma=\varinjlim_{U\ni\sigma}\mathcal F(U),$$ equals $\mathcal F(\sigma)$. The approved KO stalk (The Stal King, DRK-192) therefore coincides, for cellular sheaves, with the stalk space on the cell [E].

Proof. There are two steps.

  1. The colimit. Order the open neighbourhoods of σ by reverse inclusion. Every one of them contains the open star $\mathrm{st}(\sigma)$, which is itself open, so $\mathrm{st}(\sigma)$ is the terminal object of this directed system. A colimit over a diagram with a terminal object is the value at that object: $\mathcal F_\sigma=\mathcal F(\mathrm{st}(\sigma))$.
  2. The value on the star. For a cellular sheaf, the sections over an open set $U$ are the limit of $\mathcal F$ over the cells of $U$. In $\mathrm{st}(\sigma)$ the cell σ is a face of every other cell, so σ is initial in that diagram, and a limit over a diagram with an initial object is the value at that object: $\mathcal F(\mathrm{st}(\sigma))=\mathcal F(\sigma)$. ∎

2.4 Cochains and the coboundary

Definition 2.4. The $k$-cochains are $C^k=C^k(X;\mathcal F)=\bigoplus_{\dim\sigma=k}\mathcal F(\sigma)$, with the orthogonal direct-sum inner product. The coboundary $\delta^k:C^k\to C^{k+1}$ is $$(\delta^k x)_\tau=\sum_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau,\ \dim\sigma=k}[\sigma:\tau]\,\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}\,x_\sigma .$$ For an edge $e=(u\to v)$ this reads $$(\delta^0x)_e=\mathcal F_{v\trianglelefteq e}x_v-\mathcal F_{u\trianglelefteq e}x_u,$$ which is the disagreement of the two endpoint states on the shared edge stalk [E].

Lemma 2.5 ($\delta\delta=0$). $\delta^{1}\delta^{0}=0$.

Proof. For a face τ and a vertex ρ ≤ τ, $$(\delta^1\delta^0x)_\tau=\sum_{\rho}\Big(\sum_{\sigma}[\rho:\sigma][\sigma:\tau]\Big)\mathcal F_{\rho\trianglelefteq\tau}\,x_\rho .$$ This uses composition, $\mathcal F_{\sigma\trianglelefteq\tau}\mathcal F_{\rho\trianglelefteq\sigma}=\mathcal F_{\rho\trianglelefteq\tau}$. The inner sum vanishes by Definition 2.1. ∎ [E]

The lemma is the formal content of the slogan “local consistency is necessary for global consistency”. Every gradient datum is automatically curvature-free [E/D].

Remark 2.5a (Orientation and reciprocity). A 1-cochain is defined relative to the orientation of each edge. Reversing the orientation of $e$ negates $\omega_e$, so relational data are antisymmetric by construction: $\omega_{v\to u}=-\omega_{u\to v}$. Wrestling with God (DRK-142) reads Newton’s third law, $\mathbf F_{B\to A}=-\mathbf F_{A\to B}$, as the condition that force in grappling cannot be unilateral. In v5.0 that reading is the orientation axiom of $C^1$: an exchange is one cochain value seen from its two ends (§16.1) [E/D].

2.5 Cohomology, global sections, obstruction

Definition 2.6. The cohomology groups are $$H^0=\ker\delta^0,\qquad H^1=\ker\delta^1/\operatorname{im}\delta^0,$$ and the Betti numbers are $b_k=\dim H^k$.

Elements of $H^0$ are the global sections: vertex states that agree on every edge. This is the approved KO global section (The Operational Question in Positive Alignment, DRK-145). A nonzero class in $H^1$ is the approved KO cohomological obstruction (Drakens Ordlista, DRK-117) [E/D].

What $H^1$ obstructs needs to be stated precisely, because v4.4 and several posts state it loosely.

Principle 2.7 (Who carries $H^1$). Vertex data alone never exhibit an $H^1$ obstruction. For any $x\in C^0$, the disagreement $\delta^0x$ lies in $\operatorname{im}\delta^0$ and has zero class in $H^1$. Disagreement among vertex states is an $H^0$ phenomenon: it measures how far $x$ is from $\ker\delta^0$.

An $H^1$ obstruction appears only when the data include relational measurements $\omega\in C^1$ that are not computed from vertex states. Examples:

  • clock offsets measured on links;
  • pairwise comparisons;
  • exchange rates;
  • parallel transport of frames;
  • the signs of alliances;
  • syndromes of a code.

Each of these poses the integration problem: find $x$ with $\delta^0x=\omega$. The problem is solvable exactly when $\omega\in\operatorname{im}\delta^0$. For closed ω ($\delta^1\omega=0$) the obstruction to solving it is the class $[\omega]\in H^1$ [E].

Every $H^1$ claim in this monograph therefore names its relational datum. Claims in the corpus that attribute $H^1$ to disagreement among vertex states are restated as $H^0$ claims (Appendix C, fork F6) [D].

It persists when that restriction is stable under the dynamics of Chapter 15 [D].

3 The Hodge Spine

This chapter is the mathematical centre of v5.0. Everything in Parts II–V is a reading of, a measure on, or a dynamics for the decomposition proved here [D].

3.1 Adjoints and Hodge Laplacians

With the declared inner products (Axiom A2), each coboundary has an adjoint $\delta^{k*}$. For real stalks with orthonormal bases, $\delta^{k*}=\delta^{k\top}$.

Definition 3.0. The Hodge Laplacians of $\mathcal F$ are $$\Delta^0=\delta^{0*}\delta^0,\qquad \Delta^1=\delta^0\delta^{0*}+\delta^{1*}\delta^1,\qquad \Delta^2=\delta^1\delta^{1*}.$$ $\Delta^0$ is the sheaf Laplacian $L_{\mathcal F}$. This is the approved KO from The Operational Question in Positive Alignment (DRK-145), there written $\Delta_{\mathcal F}=\delta^\top\delta$ [E].

Its quadratic form is the total disagreement energy (Hansen & Ghrist 2019) [E]: $$E(x):=\langle x,L_{\mathcal F}x\rangle=\|\delta^0x\|^2=\sum_{e=(u\to v)}\big\|\rho_{ve}x_v-\rho_{ue}x_u\big\|^2 .$$

3.2 The decomposition

Theorem 3.1 (Hodge decomposition). For $k=0,1,2$ there is an orthogonal decomposition $$C^k=\operatorname{im}\delta^{k-1}\ \oplus\ \ker\Delta^k\ \oplus\ \operatorname{im}\delta^{k*},$$ with $\ker\Delta^k=\ker\delta^k\cap\ker\delta^{k-1*}$ and $\ker\Delta^k\cong H^k$. (Terms with out-of-range indices are zero.)

Proof.

  1. Kernel of $\Delta^k$. For any $c$, $$\langle\Delta^kc,c\rangle=\|\delta^kc\|^2+\|\delta^{k-1*}c\|^2,$$ so $\Delta^kc=0$ if and only if $\delta^kc=0$ and $\delta^{k-1*}c=0$.
  2. Orthogonality of the outer summands. By Lemma 2.5, $$\langle\delta^{k-1}a,\ \delta^{k*}b\rangle=\langle\delta^k\delta^{k-1}a,\ b\rangle=0 .$$
  3. The complement. In finite dimensions, $(\operatorname{im}\delta^{k-1})^\perp=\ker\delta^{k-1*}$ and $(\operatorname{im}\delta^{k*})^\perp=\ker\delta^k$. The orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{im}\delta^{k-1}\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta^{k*}$ is therefore $\ker\delta^{k-1*}\cap\ker\delta^k=\ker\Delta^k$.
  4. The isomorphism. Since $\operatorname{im}\delta^{k-1}\subseteq\ker\delta^k$, we have $\ker\delta^k=\operatorname{im}\delta^{k-1}\oplus\ker\Delta^k$. Hence $H^k=\ker\delta^k/\operatorname{im}\delta^{k-1}\cong\ker\Delta^k$. ∎

This is the finite-dimensional Hodge theorem for cochain complexes. For cellular sheaves it is in Hansen & Ghrist (2019); for graphs and edge flows, in Lim (2020) and Jiang et al. (2011) [E].

For edge data $\omega\in C^1$, write $\Pi_{\text{grad}},\Pi_{\text{harm}},\Pi_{\text{curl}}$ for the three orthogonal projectors. Then $$\boxed{\ \omega\;=\;\underbrace{\delta^0x^\star}_{\text{gradient}}\;+\;\underbrace{h}_{\text{harmonic}}\;+\;\underbrace{\delta^{1*}y}_{\text{curl}}\ },\qquad x^\star=(\delta^0)^{+}\omega,$$ where $^+$ is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse [E].

3.3 Energies and the integration problem

Proposition 3.2 (Energy identity; least squares). For $\omega\in C^1$:

  1. $\|\omega\|^2=E_{\text{grad}}+E_{\text{harm}}+E_{\text{curl}}$, with $E_\bullet=\|\Pi_\bullet\omega\|^2$.

  2. $\displaystyle\min_{x\in C^0}\|\omega-\delta^0x\|^2=E_{\text{harm}}+E_{\text{curl}}$.

  3. The minimisers are exactly $x^\star+H^0$.

Proof. (i) is Pythagoras for orthogonal summands. For (ii), $\delta^0x$ ranges over $\operatorname{im}\delta^0$, so the minimum is the squared distance from ω to that subspace, which is the norm of its orthogonal remainder. For (iii), $\delta^0x=\Pi_{\text{grad}}\omega$ holds if and only if $x-x^\star\in\ker\delta^0=H^0$. ∎ [E]

Definition 3.2a (Obstruction energy). $E_{\text{obs}}(\omega):=E_{\text{harm}}+E_{\text{curl}}$ is the part of the relational data that no assignment of local states can reproduce. The integrability share is $$\Gamma^1(\omega):=E_{\text{grad}}/\|\omega\|^2\in[0,1],$$ and its complement $1-\Gamma^1$ is the obstruction share [D].

3.4 Gauge invariance

A gauge transformation re-expresses every local state by an offset: $x\mapsto x+g$ for $g\in C^0$. Relational data change accordingly, $\omega\mapsto\omega+\delta^0g$. Re-zeroing every clock, re-basing every price, or re-anchoring every local convention are gauge transformations [E].

Proposition 3.3. $\Pi_{\text{harm}}$, $\Pi_{\text{curl}}$, the curvature $\delta^1\omega$, $E_{\text{harm}}$ and $E_{\text{curl}}$ are gauge invariant.

Proof. $\delta^0g\in\operatorname{im}\delta^0$ is annihilated by $\Pi_{\text{harm}}$ and $\Pi_{\text{curl}}$, and by $\delta^1$ (Lemma 2.5). ∎ [E]

Reading: no choice of local conventions can create or destroy the topological or curvature part of a disagreement. Only the gradient part is convention-dependent [E/D].

3.5 Curvature

Proposition 3.4 (Curvature determines the curl). Let $\mathcal R:=\delta^1\omega\in C^2$ be the curvature of ω. Then $$\Pi_{\text{curl}}\,\omega=\delta^{1*}\,(\delta^1\delta^{1*})^{+}\,\mathcal R,\qquad E_{\text{curl}}=\big\langle \mathcal R,\ (\Delta^2)^{+}\mathcal R\big\rangle .$$ Moreover, ω is closed ($\mathcal R=0$) if and only if its curl part vanishes.

Proof. $\delta^1$ annihilates the gradient part (Lemma 2.5) and the harmonic part (Theorem 3.1), so $\mathcal R=\delta^1\delta^{1*}y$. Choosing $y\in\operatorname{im}\delta^1$ (always possible) gives $y=(\Delta^2)^{+}\mathcal R$. Then $$E_{\text{curl}}=\langle\delta^{1*}y,\delta^{1*}y\rangle=\langle y,\Delta^2y\rangle=\langle\mathcal R,(\Delta^2)^+\mathcal R\rangle .$$ If $\mathcal R=0$ then $y=0$. Conversely, $\delta^1$ is injective on $\operatorname{im}\delta^{1*}$, so a nonzero curl part has nonzero curvature. ∎ [E]

The formula is verified numerically in Example 4.4 ($E_{\text{curl}}$ by projection and by curvature agree to $10^{-6}$) [E].

3.6 The invariant ϰ

Definition 3.5 (Draken invariant). For $\omega\in C^1$, $$\varkappa(\omega):=[\,\Pi_{\text{harm}}\,\omega\,]\in H^1(X;\mathcal F),\qquad b_1(\mathcal F)=\dim H^1 .$$

When ω is closed, $\varkappa(\omega)=[\omega]$. This is the approved KO definition $\varkappa:=[\omega]\in H^1(X,\mathcal F)$ of The Invariant (DRK-175). Definition 3.5 extends it to arbitrary relational data, which in applications are never exactly closed [E/D].

Proof of the reduction. A closed ω has no curl part (Proposition 3.4), so $\omega=\delta^0x^\star+h$ and $[\omega]=[h]$. ∎ [E]

This extension removes the inconsistency between The Invariant (DRK-175) and Ouroboros Debt (DRK-176):

  • DRK-175 requires $[\omega]$ and therefore $\delta\omega=0$.
  • DRK-176 accrues debt from $\|d\omega\|\neq 0$.

In v5.0 both hold for the same ω. The harmonic part carries ϰ; the curvature carries the curl energy, whose time integral is the curvature load $C(t)$ of Chapter 7 [D].

Proposition 3.6 (Periods). Let $\mathcal F$ be a constant sheaf $\mathbb R$ on $X$. The period of a 1-cochain on an oriented cycle γ is $\langle\omega,\gamma\rangle=\sum_{e\in\gamma}\pm\omega_e$.

  1. Periods of $h=\Pi_{\text{harm}}\omega$ depend only on the homology class of γ. They determine $\varkappa(\omega)$, since $H^1(X;\mathbb R)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_1(X),\mathbb R)$.

  2. Periods of ω itself satisfy a discrete Stokes formula: if $\gamma-\gamma'=\partial c$ for a 2-chain $c$, then $$\langle\omega,\gamma\rangle-\langle\omega,\gamma'\rangle=\langle\mathcal R,c\rangle .$$

Proof. (i) Harmonic cochains are closed, so their periods vanish on boundaries. The isomorphism is the universal coefficient theorem over a field (Hatcher 2002). (ii) $\langle\omega,\partial c\rangle=\langle\delta^1\omega,c\rangle$ by the definition of $\delta^1$ as the transpose of $\partial$. ∎ [E]

Reading: when relational data have curvature, “the discrepancy around a loop” is not well defined, because it depends on which loop represents the hole. ϰ assigns each hole a single period, the least-squares reconciliation of all representatives. In Example 4.4 the two loops around the open hole have periods 0.03 and 0.06; ϰ assigns 0.04 [E/D].

3.7 What counts as a hole is a modelling decision

Proposition 3.7 (Face filling reclassifies obstruction). Let $X'=X\cup\{\tau\}$, adding one face whose boundary is a cycle in $X$, and let $\mathcal F'$ extend $\mathcal F$. For every $\omega\in C^1$ (unchanged by the filling):

  1. $E'_{\text{grad}}=E_{\text{grad}}$;

  2. $E'_{\text{curl}}\ge E_{\text{curl}}$ and $E'_{\text{harm}}\le E_{\text{harm}}$, with $E'_{\text{harm}}+E'_{\text{curl}}=E_{\text{harm}}+E_{\text{curl}}$;

  3. $b_1'=b_1-(\operatorname{rank}\delta'^1-\operatorname{rank}\delta^1)$. For the constant scalar sheaf and a cycle that is not already a boundary, $b_1'=b_1-1$.

Proof. $C^0$, $C^1$ and $\delta^0$ are unchanged, so the gradient projection is unchanged. $\operatorname{im}\delta'^{1*}\supseteq\operatorname{im}\delta^{1*}$, and orthogonal projection onto a larger subspace has a larger norm. The sum is fixed by (i) and Proposition 3.2. Part (iii) is rank–nullity. ∎ [E]

The proposition says that whether an irreducible disagreement is topological (harmonic) or local circulation (curl) depends on whether the region it encircles is part of the declared base. An unmeasured interior is a hole. A measured interior is a source of curvature. This is why Axiom A2 requires the faces to be declared, and it resolves an ambiguity in the corpus’s physics posts (Chapter 10) [D].

3.8 Coefficients and torsion

Remark 3.8. Over a field of characteristic zero, torsion in integral homology disappears. The Klein bottle $K$ has $H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2$, but $H_1(K;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R$; the $\mathbb Z/2$ class, which is the failure of orientability, is invisible to real-valued measurement (Hatcher 2002) [E].

The Bottle Has No Outside (DRK-187) reads non-orientability as the absence of a view from outside. The precise residue of that reading is Axiom A4: obstructions of sign or orientation type must be measured with $O(1)$-valued restriction maps (signed sheaves, Section 4.7) or over $\mathbb F_2$ (codes, Chapter 12) [D].

3.9 Two faces of $H^1$

The same group plays two roles, and it is reached by two roads (integration, here, and lifting, §3.10):

  • In an integration problem (clocks, prices, comparisons), a nonzero class is what cannot be made consistent. It is an obstruction.
  • In a code (Chapter 12), a nonzero class is what local errors cannot change without crossing the code distance. It is protected information: the number of logical qubits of a CSS code is $\dim H_1$ (Calderbank & Shor 1996; Kitaev 2003).

Draken’s normative stance follows the second face as well as the first. A healthy system keeps a bounded, named, nonzero ϰ: irreducible difference that is held rather than erased (The Invariant, DRK-175, §5) [E/D].

3.10 Extensions: the second road to $H^1$

Principle 2.7 reaches $H^1$ through the integration problem: can relational data be integrated into local states? The corpus reaches the same group by a second road, the lifting problem of The Cloven Section (DRK-174): can a visible quotient be lifted back into the whole it was cut from? This section shows that the two roads meet. Every lifting obstruction is the integration obstruction of a derived relational datum, and the Hodge spine applies to it unchanged [D].

Definition 3.9 (Short exact sequence of sheaves). A sequence of cellular sheaves on $X$, $$0\longrightarrow\mathcal K\xrightarrow{\ i\ }\mathcal E\xrightarrow{\ p\ }\mathcal Q\longrightarrow0,$$ is short exact if two conditions hold:

  • on every cell σ, $0\to\mathcal K(\sigma)\to\mathcal E(\sigma)\to\mathcal Q(\sigma)\to0$ is exact;
  • $i$ and $p$ commute with all restriction maps.

DRK-174 uses three readings of the terms:

  • $p$ is the cut, clēofan;
  • $\mathcal K$ is what the cut forgets;
  • a section $s$ with $p\circ s=\mathrm{id}$ is the re-adhering, clifian [E/D].

Theorem 3.10 (Long exact sequence). A short exact sequence of cellular sheaves induces the exact sequence $$0\to H^0(\mathcal K)\to H^0(\mathcal E)\to H^0(\mathcal Q)\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }H^1(\mathcal K)\to H^1(\mathcal E)\to H^1(\mathcal Q)\to\cdots .$$ The connecting map is computed as follows. Lift $q\in H^0(\mathcal Q)$ stalkwise to some $e\in C^0(\mathcal E)$. Then $\delta_{\mathcal E}e$ lies in $i\,C^1(\mathcal K)$, and $\partial q=[\,i^{-1}\delta_{\mathcal E}e\,]$.

Proof. Stalkwise exactness and compatibility with restriction maps give a short exact sequence of cochain complexes. The snake lemma then yields the long exact sequence and the stated formula (Weibel 1994, Thm. 1.3.1). For well-definedness: $p\,\delta_{\mathcal E}e=\delta_{\mathcal Q}q=0$, so $\delta_{\mathcal E}e\in\ker p=\operatorname{im}i$ on every edge. ∎ [E]

Proposition 3.11 (Lifting is integration). Let $q\in H^0(\mathcal Q)$ and choose any stalkwise lift $e$. Put $\omega_q:=i^{-1}\delta_{\mathcal E}e\in C^1(\mathcal K)$. Then:

  1. $\omega_q$ is closed: $\delta^1_{\mathcal K}\omega_q=0$. Its curl part vanishes.

  2. The global lifts of $q$ are exactly $e-i\,k$ with $\delta^0_{\mathcal K}k=\omega_q$. Hence $q$ lifts if and only if $\varkappa(\omega_q)=\partial q=0$.

  3. A different stalkwise lift changes $\omega_q$ by a gradient. So $\varkappa(\omega_q)$ and $E_{\text{harm}}(\omega_q)$ depend on $q$ alone.

  4. If each $i_\sigma$ is an isometry, the least disagreement achievable by any lift is $$\min_{\tilde e:\ p\tilde e=q}\|\delta_{\mathcal E}\tilde e\|^2=E_{\text{harm}}(\omega_q).$$

Proof.

  1. $\delta^1_{\mathcal E}\delta^0_{\mathcal E}e=0$ by Lemma 2.5, and $i$ is injective.

  2. $p(e-ik)=q$. Moreover $\delta_{\mathcal E}(e-ik)=i(\omega_q-\delta_{\mathcal K}k)$, which vanishes if and only if $\delta_{\mathcal K}k=\omega_q$. Every lift of $q$ has this form, because two lifts differ by an element of $\ker p=\operatorname{im}i$. The class statement follows from Definition 3.5 and Theorem 3.10.

  3. Two lifts differ by $ik'$, which changes $\omega_q$ by $\delta_{\mathcal K}k'$. Apply Proposition 3.3.

  4. By (ii) and isometry, $\|\delta_{\mathcal E}(e-ik)\|^2=\|\omega_q-\delta_{\mathcal K}k\|^2$. Minimise over $k$ (Proposition 3.2) and use (i). ∎ [E]

The proposition gives the “phantom section” of DRK-174 a magnitude. A presented lift that does not commute, i.e. an apparent section of an unsplit cut, carries a disagreement of at least $E_{\text{harm}}(\omega_q)$ somewhere in the whole. A cut that “cleaves in both senses” is one with $\partial q=0$. Extraction, on DRK-174’s reading of making versus taking (Mazzucato 2018), presents $q$ as if $\partial q=0$ when it is not [D].

Two connections tie this section to the rest of the spine:

  • Since $\omega_q$ is closed, lifting obstructions are purely topological. They never carry curvature.
  • Example 4.8 computes a lifting obstruction on the ring and recovers the residual $\Phi^2/N$ of Proposition 4.1 exactly: the extension’s holonomy is the clock ring’s holonomy [E].

4.3 The clock ring: holonomy and its residue

Setting. $N=6$ clocks on a ring. Each link measures the offset between neighbours, $\omega_e$, with stalks $\mathbb R$. The true offsets are a gradient, and a uniform holonomy $\Phi=0.6$ is added: $\omega=\delta^0t+\Phi/N$ [D].

Proposition 4.1 (Ring residual). On the scalar ring with $N$ edges and no face, every ω with holonomy $\Phi=\sum_e\omega_e$ satisfies $$\min_x\|\omega-\delta^0x\|^2=\Phi^2/N .$$ The residual is entirely harmonic.

Proof. On a cycle, $\operatorname{im}\delta^0=\{g:\sum_eg_e=0\}$, which has dimension $N-1$. The orthogonal complement is spanned by $\mathbf 1$. The projection of ω onto $\mathbf 1$ is $(\Phi/N)\mathbf 1$, with squared norm $\Phi^2/N$. With no faces there is no curl. ∎ [E]

Computed. The holonomy is 0.600 and $E_{\text{harm}}=0.0600=\Phi^2/N$. When the ring’s interior is declared a face, the same 0.0600 appears entirely as $E_{\text{curl}}$ and $E_{\text{harm}}=0$, as Proposition 3.7 requires. This is the result stated, for a gravitational ring, in The Dragged Frame (DRK-171) [E].

Proposition 4.2 (U(1) ring). Let each edge transport phases by $e^{i\phi/N}$, so that the holonomy is $e^{i\phi}$. The connection Laplacian has eigenvalues $$\lambda_k=2-2\cos\!\Big(\frac{2\pi k+\phi}{N}\Big),\qquad k=0,\dots,N-1 .$$ In particular $H^0=0$ unless $\phi\in2\pi\mathbb Z$.

Proof. The Fourier modes $x_j=e^{2\pi ikj/N}$ diagonalise the circulant connection Laplacian. ∎ [E]

For $\phi=1$ the computed spectrum is $\{0.0277,0.7265,1.3012,2.6988,3.2735,3.9723\}$, matching the closed form to $10^{-6}$. No global phase assignment exists ($b_0=0$), and the best attainable spectral coherence is $1-\lambda_{\min}/\lambda_{\max}=0.993$. This is the Aharonov–Bohm ring in sheaf form (Aharonov & Bohm 1959): a flux that is invisible locally on every link and unremovable globally [E].

4.6 Kuramoto synchrony is resolvent coherence

Part II — Measures

5 Coherence Γ

The approved KO defines Γ as tracking “how well local sections glue into a globally consistent picture” (The Kaiju Manifesto, DRK-105). The corpus contains four inequivalent formulas for it:

  • v4.4: $1-R$;
  • Bootstrap Inception (DRK-185): $1-\|\delta s\|/\|s\|$;
  • The Compressible Section (DRK-157): a dimension ratio;
  • The Carrier and the Cargo (DRK-189): the order parameter.

This chapter fixes one canonical measure and derives the others as limits, bounds or special cases [D].

5.1 Definition

Definition 5.1 (Resolvent coherence). For vertex data $x\neq0$ and stiffness $\tau>0$, $$\Gamma_\tau(x)\;:=\;\frac{\big\langle x,\ (I+\tau L_{\mathcal F})^{-1}x\big\rangle}{\langle x,x\rangle}.$$ The default stiffness is $\tau^\ast:=\dim C^0/\operatorname{tr}L_{\mathcal F}$, the reciprocal mean eigenvalue. It is computed once on the reference base and then held fixed for every comparison (Axiom A2) [D].

Γ in v5.0 means $\Gamma_{\tau^\ast}$ unless another τ is declared [D].

Proposition 5.2 (Repair-cost form). $$\Gamma_\tau(x)=1-\frac{\mathcal C_\tau(x)}{\|x\|^2},\qquad \mathcal C_\tau(x):=\min_{y\in C^0}\Big\{\|y-x\|^2+\tau\,\langle y,L_{\mathcal F}y\rangle\Big\}.$$ The minimiser is $y^\ast=(I+\tau L)^{-1}x$.

Proof. Write $M=I+\tau L$, which is positive definite. Then $$\|y-x\|^2+\tau\langle y,Ly\rangle=\langle y,My\rangle-2\langle x,y\rangle+\|x\|^2 .$$ This is minimised at $y^\ast=M^{-1}x$, with minimum value $\|x\|^2-\langle x,M^{-1}x\rangle$. ∎ [E]

Reading: Γ is one minus the normalised cost of the cheapest coherent repair. The repair may change the data (cost $\|y-x\|^2$) or leave disagreement in place (cost $\tau E(y)$). The stiffness τ is the exchange rate between the two: the price of a unit of disagreement in units of a unit of data change. This is the resolvent (Tikhonov) smoother of graph signal processing (Shuman et al. 2013) applied to sheaves [E/D].

5.2 Spectral form, range and limits

Let $L=\sum_k\lambda_kP_k$ be the spectral decomposition, with $P_0$ the projector onto $\ker L=H^0$. Let $w_k=\|P_kx\|^2/\|x\|^2$, so that $\sum_kw_k=1$.

Proposition 5.3.

  1. $\displaystyle \Gamma_\tau(x)=\sum_k\frac{w_k}{1+\tau\lambda_k}$.

  2. $0\lt \Gamma_\tau(x)\le1$, with equality if and only if $x\in H^0$.

  3. $\Gamma_\tau$ is non-increasing in τ, and $$\lim_{\tau\to\infty}\Gamma_\tau(x)=\Gamma_0(x):=\frac{\|P_0x\|^2}{\|x\|^2},\qquad \Gamma_\tau(x)=1-\tau R(x)+O(\tau^2)\ \ (\tau\to0),$$ where $R(x)=E(x)/\|x\|^2$.

Proof. (i) Diagonalise $M^{-1}$. (ii) Every term is positive; $\Gamma_\tau=1$ if and only if $w_k=0$ whenever $\lambda_k>0$. (iii) Each term $1/(1+\tau\lambda_k)$ is non-increasing in τ, tends to $\mathbf 1[\lambda_k=0]$, and has derivative $-\lambda_k$ at τ = 0. ∎ [E]

Γ_τ is therefore a spectral shape of the data: a low-pass filter whose cutoff is set by τ. Shape Is Spectrum (DRK-144) reads coherence as a distribution over a basis; in v5.0 the basis is the Laplacian eigenbasis and the reading is Proposition 5.3(i) [E/D].

The dial has three settings:

  • Small τ measures only gross local disagreement.
  • Large τ measures only global agreement ($\Gamma_0$).
  • τ* sits at the system’s own mean scale.

The Kuramoto ring of Section 4.6 shows why the dial is necessary: high local agreement with no global synchrony gives $\Gamma_{\tau^\ast}=0.60$ and $\Gamma_0=0.02$ [E].

5.3 Bounds connecting the corpus’s formulas

Proposition 5.4. Let $\lambda_1^+$ and $\lambda_{\max}$ be the smallest nonzero and the largest eigenvalue. Then $$\max\Big\{\Gamma_0,\ \frac1{1+\tau R}\Big\}\ \le\ \Gamma_\tau\ \le\ \Gamma_0+\frac{1-\Gamma_0}{1+\tau\lambda_1^+},$$ $$1-\frac{R}{\lambda_1^+}\ \le\ \Gamma_0\ \le\ 1-\frac{R}{\lambda_{\max}}=:\Gamma_R .$$

Proof.

  1. $\Gamma_\tau\ge\Gamma_0$ because every term is non-negative and the $\lambda=0$ terms contribute $\Gamma_0$.
  2. $\Gamma_\tau\ge1/(1+\tau R)$ by Jensen’s inequality for the convex map $\lambda\mapsto(1+\tau\lambda)^{-1}$ under the weights $w_k$.
  3. The upper bound for $\Gamma_\tau$ replaces every nonzero eigenvalue by $\lambda_1^+$.
  4. For the second line, $R=\sum_{\lambda_k>0}w_k\lambda_k$ lies between $\lambda_1^+(1-\Gamma_0)$ and $\lambda_{\max}(1-\Gamma_0)$. ∎ [E]

How the corpus’s formulas relate to the canonical Γ:

  • The spectral form $\Gamma_R$ (DRK-189’s “normalised” formula) is an upper bound on $\Gamma_0$.
  • The unnormalised v4.4 form $1-R$ is not a coherence measure at all, since it is negative whenever $R>1$.
  • DRK-185’s $1-\|\delta s\|/\|s\|=1-\sqrt{R}$ is a monotone transform of $R$ with the same defect.

All three are retired as headline measures; $\Gamma_R$ is retained as a computable bound [D].

5.4 Invariances and the reporting duty

Proposition 5.5.

  1. $\Gamma_\tau(cx)=\Gamma_\tau(x)$ for every $c\neq0$.

  2. $\Gamma_{\tau/a}(x;aL)=\Gamma_\tau(x;L)$ for every $a>0$. Hence $\Gamma_{\tau^\ast}$ is invariant under rescaling every restriction map by the same factor.

  3. If $Q$ is a block-orthogonal change of stalk bases, with restriction maps transformed accordingly, then $\Gamma_\tau(Qx;QLQ^\top)=\Gamma_\tau(x;L)$.

Proof. (i) and (ii) are immediate from Definition 5.1, and $\tau^\ast$ scales as $1/a$. For (iii), $(I+\tau QLQ^\top)^{-1}=Q(I+\tau L)^{-1}Q^\top$. ∎ [E]

Part (iii) is the precise form of “coherence does not depend on how each node labels its own coordinates”. It underlies the drift prediction of Chapter 20 [E/D].

Proposition 5.6 (Monotonicity in the relations). Fix τ. If $L\preceq L'$ in the Loewner order, then $\Gamma_\tau(x;L')\le\Gamma_\tau(x;L)$ for every $x$. In particular:

  • adding an edge, with any restriction maps, never raises Γ_τ;
  • deleting an edge never lowers it;
  • deleting every edge gives $\Gamma_\tau\equiv1$.

Proof. $L\preceq L'$ implies $I+\tau L\preceq I+\tau L'$, and matrix inversion is order-reversing on positive-definite matrices. Adding an edge adds the positive semidefinite term $\delta_e^\top\delta_e$. ∎ [E]

Proposition 5.6 is the engine of the anti-totalisation theorem (Chapter 19). It also corrects v4.4’s “false overlap” signature (Chapter 18) [D].

Proposition 5.7 (Global-section inflation). For $s\in H^0$, $s\ne0$: $$E(x+cs)=E(x)\ \ \text{for all }c,\qquad\text{and}\qquad \Gamma_\tau(x+cs)\to1\ \ (c\to\infty).$$

Proof. $\delta^0s=0$, and the $H^0$ weight $w_0\to1$. ∎ [E]

Reporting standard 5.7a. Γ must always be reported with:

  1. the energy $E$;
  2. the declared reference $x_{\text{ref}}$ against which data are measured;
  3. the stiffness τ;
  4. the complex, stalks and restriction maps.

Example 4.1 shows a single dataset scoring 0.886 or 0.453 depending on the reference. A Γ reported alone is not admissible evidence [D].

5.5 Coherence is not soundness

Proposition 5.8 (Blindness to coherent error). Let $x=x_{\text{true}}+s$ with $s\in H^0$. Then $\delta^0x=\delta^0x_{\text{true}}$, so every function of the disagreements takes the same value on the true state and on the falsified one.

Now let anchors be added: cells $a$ carrying fixed reference data $r_a$, linked to vertices by restriction maps that together form an operator $A$. Then the falsification $s$ is detectable if and only if $As\neq0$. The undetectable subspace $$\mathcal U:=H^0\cap\ker A$$ can only shrink as anchors are added [E].

Proof. The first statement is Lemma 2.5 applied to $\delta^0s=0$. With anchors, the anchor residual $Ax-r$ changes by $As$. Adding anchors adds rows to $A$, which shrinks $\ker A$. ∎ [E]

This is the formal core of three corpus results:

  • “the synchronised lie” (The Carrier and the Cargo, DRK-189, §7);
  • the honest referee of 知行合一: When Automation Removes the Only Honest Referee (DRK-131);
  • abduction’s “coherent-but-unsound” section (The Guessed Section, DRK-170).

A system can be perfectly coherent and entirely wrong. Only restriction maps to something outside the system’s own sections, the anchors, can tell [E/D].

5.6 Estimation

Proposition 5.9 (Gradient and error propagation). With $M=I+\tau L$, $$\nabla_x\Gamma_\tau=\frac{2}{\|x\|^2}\big(M^{-1}x-\Gamma_\tau(x)\,x\big).$$ For measurement error with covariance $\Sigma_x$, the delta method gives $\sigma^2_\Gamma\approx\nabla\Gamma^\top\Sigma_x\nabla\Gamma$. The node-wise norms of $\nabla\Gamma$ rank nodes by their leverage over the score, which is also the order in which a manipulator would attack them [E].

Proof. Apply the quotient rule to $\langle x,M^{-1}x\rangle/\langle x,x\rangle$, using the symmetry of $M^{-1}$. ∎

Proposition 5.10 (Noise bias and its correction). Let $\tilde x=x+\eta$ with $\mathbb E\eta=0$ and $\operatorname{Cov}\eta=\Sigma$. Then for every symmetric $B$, $$\mathbb E\big[\langle\tilde x,B\tilde x\rangle\big]=\langle x,Bx\rangle+\operatorname{tr}(B\Sigma).$$ In particular:

  • the measured energy is inflated by $\operatorname{tr}(L\Sigma)$;
  • the naive $\Gamma_\tau$ is biased toward lower coherence;
  • the ratio estimator $$\widehat\Gamma_\tau=\frac{\langle\tilde x,M^{-1}\tilde x\rangle-\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1}\Sigma)}{\|\tilde x\|^2-\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}$$ is a ratio of unbiased estimators of the numerator and denominator, and is consistent as $\Sigma\to0$ or under averaging of repeated measurements.

Proof. $\mathbb E\langle\eta,B\eta\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(B\Sigma)$, and the cross terms vanish because $\mathbb E\eta=0$. ∎ [E]

Proposition 5.11 (Counting versus energy). For a tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, let $\sigma_\varepsilon$ be the fraction of edges with $\|(\delta^0x)_e\|\le\varepsilon$. Proof. At most $E/\varepsilon^2$ edges can carry squared disagreement greater than $\varepsilon^2$, by Markov’s inequality applied to the edge energies. ∎ [E]

They are retained as the well-posedness check that precedes Γ, and as the discrete side of soundness. They are not admissible as headline coherence [D].

5.8 The repair is a posterior mean

The resolvent of Definition 5.1 has an exact probabilistic reading. That reading connects Γ to Gaussian Markov random fields and to the denoisers of diffusion models (§15.6) [E].

Proposition 5.12 (Bayesian and score form). Give the signal $y\in C^0$ the intrinsic Gaussian Markov random field prior with precision $\tau L_{\mathcal F}$: $$p(y)\propto\exp\!\big(-\tfrac\tau2\langle y,Ly\rangle\big),$$ flat on $H^0$ (Rue & Held 2005). Observe $x=y+\xi$ with $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$. Then:

  1. $\mathbb E[y\mid x]=(I+\tau L)^{-1}x$, the repair $y^\ast$ of Proposition 5.2. Hence $$\Gamma_\tau(x)=\frac{\langle x,\ \mathbb E[y\mid x]\rangle}{\|x\|^2},$$ and the repair cost $\mathcal C_\tau(x)$ is twice the minimum of the negative log-posterior, up to an additive constant.

  2. Tweedie’s formula holds in the form $\mathbb E[y\mid x]=x+\nabla_x\log p(x)$, with $p$ the marginal density of the observation on the non-global modes (Efron 2011).

  3. The disagreement energy is a log-density: $E(x)=-\tfrac2\tau\log p(x)+\text{const}$ under the prior.

Proof.

  1. The posterior precision is $I+\tau L$ and the posterior mean is $(I+\tau L)^{-1}x$. On $H^0$ the prior precision is zero, so the mean equals the data there.

  2. In each eigenmode with $\lambda_k>0$, the marginal variance is $1+(\tau\lambda_k)^{-1}$. Then $$x_k-\frac{x_k}{1+(\tau\lambda_k)^{-1}}=\frac{x_k}{1+\tau\lambda_k},$$ which is the resolvent.

  3. Take the logarithm of the prior. ∎

The script confirms (i)–(ii) to $3\times10^{-16}$ [E].

Three consequences connect this to the rest of the monograph:

  • Γ is a model-based quantity. It measures how much of the data survives the best estimate under the prior that disagreement is improbable. τ is the prior’s precision, which is why Axiom A2 fixes it.
  • The noise correction of Proposition 5.10 and the noise floor of Proposition 18.1 are Gaussian-model statements of the same kind, and they apply under the same assumptions.
  • The repair step and the denoising step of a score-based generative model are one operation when the prior is sheaf-structured (§15.6).

6 Narrative Self-Reference Ψ and Grounding

6.1 Definition

The approved KO defines Ψ as “the ratio of narrative self-reference to reality-contact” (The Kaiju Manifesto, DRK-105). Its “psychosis metric” label is retired; the measure is structural, not clinical [D].

The operational form follows v4.4.

  • $N_S$ is the finite set of claims a system asserts in a declared corpus. A claim is an element of $N_S$; nothing outside $N_S$ is scored.
  • $O_S$ is a set of external observations, disjoint from $N_S$.
  • Each claim $c\in N_S$ has an evidential support $\operatorname{supp}(c)\subseteq N_S\cup O_S$, assigned by the protocol of §6.5.

Define $$n_{\text{self}}=|\{c\in N_S:\operatorname{supp}(c)\subseteq N_S\}|,\qquad n_{\text{contact}}=|\{c\in N_S:\operatorname{supp}(c)\cap O_S\ne\emptyset\}|,\qquad \Psi=\frac{n_{\text{self}}}{n_{\text{contact}}}.$$ Unsupported claims, with empty support, count as self-referential [D].

Lemma 6.1. Under these definitions $n_{\text{self}}+n_{\text{contact}}=|N_S|$. Hence Ψ is the odds of self-support, $$\Psi=\frac{\bar\psi}{1-\bar\psi}\in[0,\infty],\qquad \bar\psi:=\frac{n_{\text{self}}}{|N_S|}=\frac{\Psi}{1+\Psi}\in[0,1].$$

Proof. Each $c\in N_S$ falls in exactly one class. Either $\operatorname{supp}(c)\subseteq N_S$, or it contains an element outside $N_S$. Since supports lie in $N_S\cup O_S$, that element is in $O_S$. The two cases are exclusive because $N_S\cap O_S=\emptyset$. ∎ [E]

The bounded companion $\bar\psi$ reconciles the corpus’s usage:

  • “Ψ → 1” in The Protocol and the Predator (DRK-133, KO kayfabe) and in Ouroboros Debt (DRK-176) is read as $\bar\psi\to1$: almost every claim is supported only by other claims.
  • Ψ itself is unbounded and diverges in the same limit. Thresholds $\Psi_{\text{viable}}$ are stated in odds [D].

6.2 The grounding sheaf

Build a support complex:

  • vertices: claims;
  • edges: support relations between claims, carrying restriction maps that encode what one claim implies about another;
  • anchors: observations in $O_S$, attached to the claims they support.

Self-supported claims are those with no path to an anchor. By Proposition 5.8, the falsifications of the narrative that are invisible to every check, internal or external, form $\mathcal U=H^0\cap\ker A$. Adding anchors shrinks $\mathcal U$ [E/D].

Hypothesis H6.1. In observed narratives, the undetectable share $\dim\mathcal U/\dim H^0$ increases with $\bar\psi$. Ψ is then an inexpensive observable proxy for the dimension of the space of coherent lies a system could hold without internal or external alarm [H].

6.3 Ψ and Γ are independent coordinates

v4.4 conjectured that Ψ and Γ are inversely monotone (its §4.1.2). Proposition 5.8 refutes the conjecture as a law. A system can raise Γ toward 1 by adding a coherent narrative, a global section, while $\bar\psi$ rises, because nothing in Γ consults the anchors. Functional independence, meaning that neither measure determines the other, is therefore proved [E]. Whether the two are statistically correlated in real systems is a separate, empirical question [H]. The conjecture is withdrawn as a law [D].

What replaces it is a two-coordinate diagnostic:

low $\bar\psi$ (grounded) high $\bar\psi$ (self-referential)
high Γ Coherent and accountable Kayfabe / synchronised lie / egregore: locally glued, globally unanchored
low Γ Honest disagreement; repairable Fragmenting self-narratives

The dangerous quadrant is the upper right:

  • Kayfabe (KO, DRK-133) names it in the working vocabulary.
  • An egregore (KO, The Compressed Will, DRK-191) is “a well-glued section whose stability no longer depends on any single stalk” (a global section maintained by the gluing itself rather than by anchors) [D].

Whether the empirical joint distribution of (Γ, $\bar\psi$) concentrates off the anti-diagonal is an open question (Prediction P1′, Chapter 22) [H].

6.4 Observer-level self-reference

The KO synchronicity density σ(t) (The Common Base, DRK-177) is “the fraction of salient events at time t that the observer reads as addressed to the self”. It is the observer-level analogue of $\bar\psi$: interpretation supported only by self-reference [D].

Its methodological companion is base-rate hygiene. With $N\approx10^6$ salient events in 35 days and coincidence probability $p\approx10^{-6}$, the expected number of “unreasonable” coincidences is $Np\approx1$ per month (computed in DRK-177; the one-event-per-month heuristic is the conventional gloss on Littlewood’s Miscellany (Littlewood 1986), not a theorem in it). A σ(t) excess must be computed against such a base rate before it is read [E/D].

The The Ouroboros Debt (DRK-176) basin share $\beta_{\text{self}}=\mu(B(A_{\text{self}}))/\mu(X)$ is renamed from Ψ to $\beta_{\text{self}}$. It is the dynamical analogue: the share of state space that flows into the self-model’s attractor. A monotone relation between $\beta_{\text{self}}$ and $\bar\psi$ is hypothesised, not derived [H].

6.5 Coding protocol for Ψ

Ψ is measurable only under a protocol a second person can apply. v5.0 specifies the following minimum [D].

  1. Corpus. Declare the documents, the period and the issuing system $S$ in advance.
  2. Claim unit. A claim is a declarative clause asserting a proposition about $S$ or its situation that could in principle be false. Questions, commands, hedged speculation and pure evaluation without a factual component are excluded. Each claim is entered once; repetitions are de-duplicated by meaning.
  3. Support assignment. $\operatorname{supp}(c)$ is everything the text itself offers as evidence for $c$: explicit citations, references, data, quotations and inferential markers (“because”, “as shown by”) within the same paragraph or a referenced appendix.
    • Each item is coded internal if it is another claim, statement or document of $S$ itself.
    • Each item is coded external if it is an independently produced observation: third-party data, measurements, audited records or independent reports.
    • Mixed items are split. Nothing outside the text is used.
  4. Reliability. Two coders code a random 20% sample independently. Ψ is reported only if Cohen’s κ ≥ 0.70 on the internal/external decision (Landis & Koch 1977). Otherwise the codebook is revised and the sample recoded.
  5. Uncertainty. Report $\bar\psi$ with a bootstrap interval over claims, and Ψ derived from it.

Automated coding (NLP extraction of claims and support) is admissible only after it reaches κ ≥ 0.70 against the human coders on the same sample. Until a corpus has been coded this way, the computable debt in this monograph is $K_\Gamma$ (§7.3), not $K$ [D].

7 The Debt Calculus

7.1 One functional, four debts

The corpus contains several integrals called “debt”. They share one form.

Definition 7.1 (Ratchet functional). For a locally integrable rate $f:[0,T]\to\mathbb R$ and a bounded weight $w\ge0$, $$\mathcal D[f,w](t):=\int_0^t\big[f(s)\big]^+\,w(s)\,ds,\qquad [a]^+=\max(a,0).$$

Proposition 7.2. $\mathcal D$ has the following properties:

  1. $\mathcal D(0)=0$, and $\mathcal D$ is non-decreasing and absolutely continuous.

  2. It is additive over consecutive intervals.

  3. $\mathcal D[f,w](t)=0$ if and only if $f\le0$ almost everywhere on $\{w>0\}\cap[0,t]$.

  4. It is monotone in $f$ and positively homogeneous.

  5. It is subadditive: $\mathcal D[f+g,w]\le\mathcal D[f,w]+\mathcal D[g,w]$.

Proof. (i)–(iv) are properties of the Lebesgue integral of a non-negative integrand. (v) follows from $[a+b]^+\le[a]^++[b]^+$. ∎ [E]

Subadditivity has a reading. Splitting one excess into two cannot increase the debt, and two excesses that partly cancel accrue less debt together than apart. Debt penalises net excess at each instant, not gross activity [E/D].

7.2 Gross debt and outstanding balance

v4.4 said both that K “accumulates monotonically” and that it “can only be reduced through structural intervention”. As written, K is monotone by construction and cannot be reduced at all. v5.0 resolves this by separating two quantities [D]:

  • $K(t)$ is the gross accrued debt, monotone.
  • The outstanding balance is $$B_K(t):=K(t)-P_K(t),$$ where $P_K$ is the non-decreasing cumulative discharge recorded at correction events: reforms, reintroductions, repairs, or (in the code picture of Section 12.3) decoder corrections.

Two diagnostics follow. A system with high $K$ and low $B_K$ has paid its bills. A system with high $B_K$ is running a deficit that, on the Draken reading, is discharged eventually. The discharge is either deliberate or forced by a correction event, which Ouroboros Debt (DRK-176) models as a basin-boundary metamorphosis (Grebogi, Ott & Yorke 1987) [D].

Hypothesis H7.1 (Masked obstruction). Coherence debt accrues fastest when a system reports an obstruction as resolved. 7.5 Care and variety

The care operator (Norm N2) needs a variable.

Let $\mathcal V$ be variety: the logarithm of the number of distinguishable states of a system (Ashby 1956), estimated operationally as the Shannon entropy of its observed state distribution. Following The Countable Mouth (DRK-181), split its rate of change as $$\dot{\mathcal V}=\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{endo}}+\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{exo}}:$$ [D]

  • $\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{endo}}$ is variety generated within the system (mutation, invention);
  • $\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{exo}}$ is the change the agent causes in the variety of what it is coupled to [D].

The approved KO states care as $\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{exo}}=0$ (The Bridge and the Burn, DRK-162). Ouroboros Debt (DRK-176) writes the care condition as $\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{exo}}\ge0$. v5.0 reconciles the two as follows [D]:

  • Care is the zero-contribution constraint. The agent neither destroys nor homogenises what it is bound to; this covers imposing its own form on others, which raises its share of their state while lowering their variety.
  • Accounting is asymmetric. Only the destructive side accrues trophic debt, $\Theta=\mathcal D[-\dot{\mathcal V}_{\text{exo}},1]$.

The optimisation axiom N1 constrains the system’s own diversity ($dH/dt\ge0$). N2 constrains its effect on others’ variety. Together they forbid both self-simplification and export of simplification [D].

Definition 8.1 (Path abstraction depth). Definition 8.2. $\alpha^\ast(u,v):=\min_{\gamma:u\to v}\alpha(\gamma)$.

9 The Invariant and the Warded Triple

9.1 Metrics versus invariant

The Invariant (DRK-175) distinguishes two species of quantity:

  • metrics (Γ, Ψ, K): continuous, frame-dependent energy functionals;
  • the invariant ϰ: discrete, preserved under deformation that does not change the base.

The Hodge spine makes the distinction exact. Metrics are norms of projections. ϰ is a cohomology class, invariant under gauge (Proposition 3.3) and changed only by changing the complex (Proposition 3.7) [E/D].

The coherence genus is $b_1=\dim H^1$, the number of independent irreducible ways local relations fail to integrate. For the constant sheaf $\mathbb R^d$ on a graph, $$b_1=d\,(|E|-|V|+c),$$ where $c$ is the number of components [E].

On the justification offered in DRK-175 for calling ϰ the invariant:

  • The linking number of two curves is an $H^1$ pairing: the class of one curve in $H_1$ of the other’s complement, by Alexander duality (Hatcher 2002) [E].
  • The Jones polynomial and Chern–Simons invariants (Witten 1989) are not degree-1 cohomology classes, and Chern numbers live in $H^2$ (Thouless et al. 1982).

The analogy to knot theory therefore holds for linking but not beyond it. The v5.0 claim is limited accordingly [E/D].

9.2 The warded triple

The approved KO (The Warded Class, DRK-190) proposes $$\mathfrak W(\mathcal F):=(b_1,\ d,\ \rho_s):$$

  • the rank $b_1$;
  • the (co)systole $d$: the least weight of a cocycle that is not a coboundary, i.e. how much local damage flips a class;
  • the soundness $\rho_s$: whether local checks register damage in proportion to its size.

The KO writes soundness as ρ. v5.0 writes $\rho_s$ to keep ρ for restriction maps (Appendix A) [D].

DRK-190, §9, identifies a gap: $d$ and $\rho_s$ need a weight on real cochains before they can be measured on Draken sheaves. v5.0 closes the gap for soundness [D].

Proposition 9.1 (Spectral soundness). For every $f\in C^1(X;\mathcal F)$ over $\mathbb R$, $$\|\delta^1f\|\ \ge\ s_1\cdot\operatorname{dist}(f,Z^1),\qquad s_1:=\sqrt{\lambda^+_{\min}(\Delta^2)},$$ where $Z^1=\ker\delta^1$ and $\lambda^+_{\min}$ is the smallest nonzero eigenvalue. The constant is sharp.

Proof.

  1. By Theorem 3.1, $f-\Pi_{Z^1}f=\Pi_{\text{curl}}f\in\operatorname{im}\delta^{1*}=(\ker\delta^1)^\perp$.
  2. On that subspace, $\|\delta^1v\|\ge s_1\|v\|$, where $s_1$ is the smallest nonzero singular value of $\delta^1$.
  3. Equality holds for the corresponding singular vector. ∎ [E]

$s_1$ is the ℓ²-analogue of the coboundary-expansion constant of Linial & Meshulam (2006) and Gromov (2010), as reported in DRK-190 [E/D]:

  • a sheaf with $s_1$ bounded below raises alarm in proportion to damage;
  • $s_1\to0$ is silent drift.

The systole $d$ remains defined only over $\mathbb F_2$ (codes), or over a declared integer lattice of periods. A real-valued instrument for $d$ is an open problem [D].

Normative corollary 9.2. Interventions should keep $b_1>0$ and bounded, raise $d$, and keep $s_1$ bounded below. 9.5 Creating held difference

Excision lowers $b_1$ (Theorem 19.2(iv)). The inverse operation, attaching cells, can raise it. The cleanest example is a change of surface genus. For the constant sheaf $\mathbb R$: $$H^1(S^2;\mathbb R)=0,\qquad H^1(T^2;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R^2 .$$ A sphere holds no irreducible circulation. A torus surface holds two independent ones, which are exactly the two logical qubits of the toric code (Example 4.5). A solid torus (a ball with one tunnel) holds one: $H^1(D^2\times S^1;\mathbb R)\cong\mathbb R$ [E].

The Sonder Egg (DRK-166) reads gastrulation as such a change. That a through-gut is a tunnel through the body is anatomy (Barresi & Gilbert 2023) [S]. Which of these is the right base for any Draken reading of development is not established [H]. The Draken reading is that development creates $b_1$: it opens a channel through which difference can be held, where excision closes one [D].

10.2 Synchronisation is a connection

In a stationary spacetime, an observer can synchronise a clock with a neighbouring clock. The correction to be applied is $$\Delta t=-\frac{g_{0i}}{g_{00}}\,dx^i .$$ Synchronisation around a closed contour is in general impossible, because the corrections do not sum to zero (Landau & Lifshitz 1975, §84) [E].

Define the synchronisation 1-form $\omega_{\text{sync}}=-(g_{0i}/g_{00})\,dx^i$; sign conventions vary and do not affect exactness. A global synchronisation is a function $t_0$ with $dt_0=\omega_{\text{sync}}$. It exists if and only if $\omega_{\text{sync}}$ is exact. This is the integration problem of Principle 2.7 [E].

Discretisation. A network of clocks is a 1-complex:

  • vertices are clocks;
  • edges are links on which the offset $\omega_e$ is measured by two-way exchange;
  • the stalks are $\mathbb R$.

A global time assignment exists if and only if $\omega\in\operatorname{im}\delta^0$. The irreducible residual is $E_{\text{obs}}$ (Proposition 3.2) [E].

Prior engineering work. Integrating relative clock offsets over a network and attributing what cannot be integrated to cycles is standard in distributed clock synchronisation (Giridhar & Kumar 2006). Freris, Graham & Kumar (2011) show that when link delays are unknown, clock offsets cannot be uniquely determined from timestamp exchanges, and they characterise what can be. Estimating node values from noisy relative measurements has error variance governed by effective resistance in the measurement graph (Barooah & Hespanha 2008). The Draken contribution here is the reading, and the link to the rest of the spine, not the mathematics [E/D].

Conventionality as gauge. Reichenbach’s ε-conventionality of distant simultaneity is the freedom to choose the one-way split of a two-way light time (Reichenbach 1928). That freedom adds a gradient $\delta^0g$ to ω. By Proposition 3.3 it changes neither the curvature nor ϰ. The physical content of synchronisation is the gauge-invariant part, which is the reading of The One-Way Gauge (DRK-168) [E/D].

Sagnac. In a frame rotating with angular velocity Ω, the desynchronisation accumulated around a loop enclosing area $A$ is $2\Omega A/c^2$ to first order. The interferometric Sagnac delay between counter-propagating beams is twice this, $4\Omega A/c^2$ (Post 1967) [E].

Correction to the corpus. The rotating disc is simply connected. Its synchronisation obstruction is therefore curvature (curl), $\nabla\times(g_{0i}/g_{00})\ne0$, not a topological class. It appears as a harmonic class only when the interior of the loop is excluded from the measured base, as for a ring of clocks around an unmeasured centre (Proposition 3.7). DRK-168’s “the obstruction lives in $H^1$” is correct for the ring and incorrect for the disc [E/D].

10.3 Frame-dragging

To first order in a weak, stationary field ($G=c=1$), $$ds^2=-(1+2\Phi_N)\,dt^2+2A_i\,dx^i\,dt+(1-2\Phi_N)\,\delta_{ij}\,dx^idx^j .$$ A rotating mass of angular momentum $\mathbf J$ produces the gravitomagnetic potential $\mathbf A\propto\mathbf J\times\mathbf r/r^3$ (Lense & Thirring 1918). Normalisation conventions for gravitomagnetism differ by constant factors [E].

Then $\omega_{\text{sync}}\approx\pm A_i\,dx^i$. By Stokes, the desynchronisation around a loop is the flux of $\mathbf B_g=\nabla\times\mathbf A$ through any surface it bounds. That flux is the curvature of the sync form [E].

For a ring of $N$ clocks with holonomy $\Phi$ and no measured interior, the irreducible residual is $\Phi^2/N$ (Proposition 4.1). This is the inequality of The Dragged Frame (DRK-171), §2, in its exact discrete form [E/D].

DRK-171’s further readings, of the horizon as a convergence obstruction and of the information paradox as debt, are analogies [H]. They are not used in v5.0.

11 Gauge, Berry and Holonomy

11.1 Berry’s phase as a U(1) sheaf

A quantum eigenstate $|n(\mathbf R)\rangle$ transported adiabatically around a closed loop $\mathcal C$ in parameter space acquires the geometric phase $$\gamma_n(\mathcal C)=\oint_{\mathcal C}\mathbf A_n\cdot d\mathbf R=\iint_S\boldsymbol\Omega_n\cdot d\mathbf S,\qquad \mathbf A_n=i\langle n|\nabla_{\mathbf R}n\rangle$$ (Berry 1984; Simon 1983) [E].

Its discrete, gauge-invariant form for states $|u_1\rangle,\dots,|u_N\rangle=|u_1\rangle$ sampled along the loop is $$\gamma=-\operatorname{Im}\ln\prod_k\langle u_k|u_{k+1}\rangle$$ (Resta 2000) [E].

Proposition 11.1 (Discrete Berry phase is sheaf holonomy). Build a sheaf on the sampled loop as follows:

  • stalks $\mathbb C$;
  • the edge $k\to k+1$ transports by the unit phase of $\langle u_k|u_{k+1}\rangle$.

This is a U(1) sheaf whose holonomy is $e^{-i\gamma}$. It has a nonzero global section if and only if $\gamma\in2\pi\mathbb Z$. Its connection-Laplacian spectrum is that of Proposition 4.2 with $\phi=-\gamma$ (for uniform transport) [E].

Proof. The product of transports around the loop is $\prod_k\langle u_k|u_{k+1}\rangle/|\cdot|=e^{-i\gamma}$. Apply Proposition 4.2 after a gauge transformation that makes the transports uniform. ∎ [E]

The Aharonov–Bohm effect is the special case of an external flux through the ring (Aharonov & Bohm 1959) [E].

Holonomic robustness. The phase depends on the loop’s geometry, not on the speed at which it is traversed. Holonomic quantum computation exploits this robustness (Zanardi & Rasetti 1999) [E]. Picking Berries (DRK-172) reads archetypes as transported eigenstates. That reading is an analogy [M], and only the mathematics enters v5.0.

11.2 Non-abelian holonomy

For a degenerate eigenspace of dimension $n$, transport is by the path-ordered exponential of a $\mathfrak u(n)$-valued connection. The holonomy is a unitary matrix (Wilczek & Zee 1984) [E].

As a sheaf, the stalks are $\mathbb C^n$ and the transports are unitary. $H^0$ is the subspace fixed by every holonomy. Its dimension is the number of independent states that return to themselves around every loop [E].

11.3 Frustration and the coherence ceiling

For any connection sheaf (orthogonal or unitary transports), there is a ceiling on attainable coherence: $$\max_x\Gamma_R(x)=1-\lambda_{\min}/\lambda_{\max} .$$

  • If the holonomy is trivial, $\lambda_{\min}=0$ and the ceiling is 1.
  • Otherwise $\lambda_{\min}>0$, and no assignment of local states achieves full coherence.

Bandeira, Singer & Spielman (2013) bound $\lambda_{\min}$ of the graph connection Laplacian above and below by a combinatorial frustration constant, a Cheeger-type inequality. Singer & Wu (2012) developed the connection Laplacian for data on manifolds [E].

Reading: in a system whose relations carry transports (translations, conversions, sign conventions), the topology of those transports sets a hard ceiling on achievable coherence. No amount of local effort can exceed it. The only remedies are to change the transports or the base [E/D].

12 Codes: Where $H^1$ Is Protected

12.1 CSS codes are chain complexes

A chain complex over $\mathbb F_2$, $C_2\xrightarrow{\partial_2}C_1\xrightarrow{\partial_1}C_0$ with $\partial_1\partial_2=0$, defines a CSS code as follows:

  • qubits are placed on $C_1$;
  • the X-checks are $H_X=\partial_2^\top$ and the Z-checks are $H_Z=\partial_1$;
  • the checks commute because $H_XH_Z^\top=(\partial_1\partial_2)^\top=0$;
  • the number of logical qubits is $$k=\dim H_1=n-\operatorname{rank}\partial_1-\operatorname{rank}\partial_2$$

(Calderbank & Shor 1996; Steane 1996; Kitaev 2003) [E].

The toric code on an $L\times L$ torus has $k=2$ and distance $d=L$. This is computed for $L=3,4$ in Example 4.5 [E].

12.2 Tanner codes are global sections

A Tanner code places a local code on each vertex. A word is valid when its restriction to every vertex neighbourhood lies in the local code; the code is therefore $H^0$ of a sheaf. Panteleev & Kalachev (2024) define sheaf codes in exactly these terms. Quantum Tanner codes (Leverrier & Zémor 2022) and asymptotically good quantum LDPC codes (Panteleev & Kalachev 2022) are built from such structures over expanding complexes [E].

Later constructions reported in The Warded Class (DRK-190) are unreviewed preprints [S]:

  • cubical sheaf complexes with constant soundness (Chen et al. 2026);
  • explicit good quantum locally testable codes (Gay & Jeronimo 2026).

Exactness of the bridge. Placing qubits in degree 1 of a sheaf cochain complex gives $k=\dim H^1(X;\mathcal F)=b_1$. The coherence genus of a Draken sheaf and the number of logical qubits of a sheaf code are the same functor applied to different sheaves [E/D].

12.3 Locality forces fragility

For codes whose checks are geometrically local in two dimensions, $kd^2=O(n)$ (Bravyi, Poulin & Terhal 2010). Many protected differences can each be erased cheaply unless connectivity is non-local [E].

Read through the warded triple, flat locality forces the “fragile plurality” regime. Holding many differences robustly requires expansion: relations that reach beyond the local (The Warded Class, DRK-190, §§4–5) [D].

The toric code’s soundness tends to zero. A string error of length ℓ has syndrome weight 2 regardless of ℓ, so damage accumulates silently until it crosses $d/2$. This is the coding form of silent debt (Section 7.4) [E/D].

13 Oscillators, Beliefs and Signed Relations

13.1 Kuramoto

The Kuramoto model is $$\dot\theta_i=\omega_i+\frac KN\sum_jA_{ij}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i) .$$ For all-to-all coupling with a symmetric unimodal frequency density $g$, synchrony onsets at $K_c=2/(\pi g(0))$ (Kuramoto 1984; Strogatz 2000; Acebrón et al. 2005) [E].

For identical frequencies the dynamics are the gradient flow of $$U(\theta)=-\frac{K}{2N}\sum_{ij}A_{ij}\cos(\theta_j-\theta_i) .$$ Linearised about synchrony, they become $\dot\theta=-\tfrac KN L\theta$: sheaf diffusion on the constant sheaf (Chapter 15) [E].

Carrier and cargo. The approved KOs separate two quantities (The Carrier and the Cargo, DRK-189):

  • coordination (carrier): temporal alignment;
  • representational alignment (cargo): holding the same belief vector.

In v5.0 they are two sheaves on the same interaction graph [E/D]:

  • The carrier sheaf has scalar U(1) stalks. Its coherence is $\Gamma_0=r^2$ (Proposition 4.3).
  • The cargo sheaf has stalks $\mathbb R^d$ and estimated restriction maps. Its coherence is $\Gamma_\tau$ of the belief data.

Because the stalks differ in dimension, a one-dimensional carrier cannot represent content. Phase synchrony is uninformative about what is aligned (Varlet & Grootswagers 2024) [S/D]. Phase coupling between brains is measured by the phase-locking value (Lachaux et al. 1999) [E].

Hypothesis H13.1 (Dissociation). Carrier coherence and cargo coherence can be driven independently. Hyperscanning studies that measure both will find the four combinations, including high-carrier/low-cargo (“in sync about nothing”) and high-carrier/high-cargo/low-anchoring (the synchronised lie of Proposition 5.8) [H].

DRK-189 also proposes a swarmalator-type cross-gating between the two layers, in which agreement raises coupling and coupling raises transfer (O’Keeffe, Hong & Strogatz 2017). It is retained as a model to be tested, not a result [H].

13.2 Bounded confidence and fragmentation

In bounded-confidence dynamics, agents interact only with those whose beliefs lie within ε of their own (Hegselmann & Krause 2002). The interaction complex is therefore state-dependent. As beliefs separate, edges vanish and $b_0$ rises [E].

Fragmentation is an increase in $b_0$, the number of components that can each be internally coherent. It is detected spectrally by the approach of $\lambda_1^+$ to zero (Fiedler 1973) [E/D].

13.3 Signed relations

Antagonistic interactions are modelled by signed restriction maps (Altafini 2013). By Proposition 4.4:

  • bipartite consensus, two opposed camps, is a global section of a balanced signed sheaf;
  • frustration is nontrivial $O(1)$ holonomy (Harary 1953).

15.1 Sheaf diffusion

The simplest dynamics consistent with the Hodge spine is gradient descent on disagreement energy. It is the heat equation on the sheaf: $$\dot x=-\kappa L_{\mathcal F}x,\qquad \kappa>0 .$$ Hansen & Ghrist (2021) analyse it as opinion dynamics on discourse sheaves [E].

v4.4 also derived the same equation from active inference. Each node descends its local variational free energy under Gaussian generative models with precision Π, which yields $\dot x=-\Pi Lx$ in the isotropic case (Friston 2010). That derivation is retained with status [S].

Theorem 15.1 (Convergence). Let $P$ project onto $H^0$ and $x(t)=e^{-\kappa Lt}x_0$. Then:

  1. $Px(t)=Px_0$ for all $t$. Diffusion never changes the global section.

  2. $\|(I-P)x(t)\|\le e^{-\kappa\lambda_1^+t}\,\|(I-P)x_0\|$.

  3. If $Px_0\neq0$, then $1-\Gamma_0(x(t))\le e^{-2\kappa\lambda_1^+t}\,\|(I-P)x_0\|^2/\|Px_0\|^2$.

Proof. Expand $x_0$ in the eigenbasis of $L$. Kernel components are constant and every other component decays at rate at least $\kappa\lambda_1^+$. For (iii), $1-\Gamma_0=\|(I-P)x\|^2/(\|Px\|^2+\|(I-P)x\|^2)\le\|(I-P)x\|^2/\|Px_0\|^2$. ∎ [E]

The relaxation time is $1/(\kappa\lambda_1^+)$. For the institutional sheaf (Example 4.1) with κ = 1 it is 1.93 reporting cycles. Diffusion resolves disagreement toward the projection of the initial state, and never toward truth. Combined with Proposition 5.8: consensus dynamics converge to whatever global section the initial data contained, including a false one [E/D].

Proposition 15.2 (Forced diffusion). For $\dot x=-\kappa Lx+f(t)$ with $\sup_t\|(I-P)f\|\le F$: $$Px(t)=Px_0+\int_0^tPf\,ds,\qquad \limsup_{t\to\infty}\|(I-P)x(t)\|\le\frac{F}{\kappa\lambda_1^+}.$$

Proof. Variation of constants applied separately on $H^0$ and on its complement. ∎ [E]

Two consequences follow. Forcing along global sections accumulates without restoring force: a whole system can drift together with no internal alarm. Forcing against global sections is held to an amplitude inversely proportional to the spectral gap [E/D].

15.2 Relaxation of relational data

Theorem 15.3 (Irreducible residual). The gradient flow of $\tfrac12\|\delta^0x-\omega\|^2$, $$\dot x=-\kappa\,\delta^{0*}(\delta^0x-\omega),$$ converges to $x_\infty=x^\star+Px_0$. The residual converges to $$\delta^0x(t)-\omega\ \longrightarrow\ -(h+\delta^{1*}y),$$ the harmonic and curl parts of ω. The residual energy decreases monotonically to $E_{\text{obs}}(\omega)$, exponentially at rate at least $2\kappa\lambda_1^+$.

Proof. Write $x=x^\star+Px_0+u$ with $u\perp H^0$. Since $\delta^{0*}\omega=\delta^{0*}\delta^0x^\star$ (the harmonic and curl parts lie in $\ker\delta^{0*}$), we get $\dot u=-\kappa Lu$. Apply Theorem 15.1 to $u$ and Proposition 3.2 to the residual. ∎ [E]

Theorem 15.3 is the dynamical content of the Hodge spine. Local relaxation is as effective as it can be, and it stops exactly at the obstruction. What remains is invariant under every local effort (Proposition 3.3). It is what a system must either hold (as ϰ), repair by changing its base or transports, or deny, which accrues debt by Hypothesis H7.1 [E/D].

15.3 Time-varying restriction maps

When the maps change with time, $\dot x=-\kappa L(t)x+f$ is non-autonomous. If they change slowly compared with the relaxation rate, the state tracks the instantaneous kernel (adiabatic regime). If they change faster, coherence never establishes [H].

The precise adiabatic condition depends on the gap $\lambda_1^+(t)$ and the rate $\|\dot L\|$. A rule of thumb is $\|\dot L\|\ll(\kappa\lambda_1^+)^2$. This is stated as a working hypothesis, not a proved bound [H].

15.4 Critical slowing down

Proposition 15.4. For the stochastic diffusion $dx=-\kappa Lx\,dt+\sigma\,dW$, each nonzero eigenmode $k$ is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with:

  • stationary variance $\sigma^2/(2\kappa\lambda_k)$;
  • lag-Δ autocorrelation $e^{-\kappa\lambda_k\Delta}$.

As $\lambda_1^+\to0$, the variance and autocorrelation of the slowest mode diverge.

Proof. The modes decouple; each is an OU process with mean-reversion rate $\kappa\lambda_k$. ∎ [E]

16.6 Iteration and closure: the productile monad

The Productile Monad (DRK-140) reads a self-iterating protocol as a monad. Proposition 16.5 (Repair converges to the idempotent). Let $R_\tau=(I+\tau L)^{-1}$ and let $P$ be the orthogonal projection onto $H^0$. Then:

  1. $P^2=P=P^\ast$;

  2. $PR_\tau=R_\tau P=P$;

  3. $R_\tau^k\to P$ as $k\to\infty$, and $e^{-\kappa Lt}\to P$ as $t\to\infty$;

  4. $R_\tau$ is idempotent only if $L=0$.

Proof. Diagonalise. $R_\tau$ multiplies mode $k$ by $(1+\tau\lambda_k)^{-1}$, which equals 1 on $H^0$ and lies strictly in $(0,1)$ otherwise. Powers of such a factor tend to 0, and $R_\tau^2=R_\tau$ forces every factor to be 0 or 1. ∎ [E]

17 Inference, Belief and Action

17.1 Abduction is a lift

Let $O:C^0\to Y$ be the observation operator: the restriction of the full state to what is observed. Given an observation $y$, the hypotheses consistent with it form the affine space $x_0+\ker O$ [E].

  • Deduction pushes forward along $O$.
  • Abduction chooses a lift: a point of the fibre $O^{-1}(y)$.
  • Bayesian abduction chooses the lift that maximises the posterior.

(The Guessed Section, DRK-170) [E/D].

The fibre is not resolved by the data. Two lifts that differ by an element of $\ker O\cap H^0$ are equally coherent and equally consistent with every observation; this is the undetectable subspace $\mathcal U$ of Proposition 5.8 with $A=O$. Abduction is the home of the coherent-but-unsound because its output space is a fibre whose kernel is invisible by construction [E/D].

The more an inference engine fills that fibre with fluent, globally coherent lifts, the more weight falls on anchors that the engine cannot itself supply. This includes current language models, read in DRK-170, §7, as mechanised abduction [H].

Proposition 18.2 (Too good to be true). Let a vertex’s report carry honest independent noise of covariance $\sigma^2I$ while its neighbours are held fixed. Then its local energy $E_v=\sum_{e\ni v}\|(\delta^0x)_e\|^2$ satisfies $$\mathbb E\,E_v\ \ge\ \sigma^2\operatorname{tr}\Big(\sum_{e\ni v}\rho_{ve}^\top\rho_{ve}\Big).$$

Proof. Apply Proposition 5.10 to the local energy with only $x_v$ perturbed. The cross term vanishes in expectation, and the noise-free term is non-negative. ∎ [E]

A local energy persistently far below this floor is evidence that the report was constructed from the neighbours rather than measured. In honest noisy data, perfect agreement is anomalous. The test is a sheaf analogue of forensic tests for fabricated data [E/D].

v4.4 claimed that substitution produces “coherence anomalies two or more hops away”. That is not true in general; on a tree no such anomaly can arise. Distant anomalies appear only when the substituted vertex lies on a cycle whose holonomy the substitution changes, or on a path to an anchor [E].

19 The Anti-Totalisation Theorem

19.1 Base reduction

Definition 19.1 (Base reduction). A base reduction replaces $(X,\mathcal F)$ by $(X',\mathcal F')$ through any of the following:

  1. deleting edges, together with the faces that contain them;

  2. deleting faces;

  3. zeroing edge stalks ($\mathcal F(e)\mapsto0$);

  4. deleting vertices, together with their incident cells.

Operations (a) and (c) are relation excision. Operation (d) is stalk excision (The Burnt Section, DRK-158) [D].

19.2 The theorem

Theorem 19.2 (Anti-totalisation). Fix τ and the vertex data $x$. Then:

  1. Relation excision weakly increases $\Gamma_\tau(x)$ and $\Gamma_0(x)$, and weakly decreases $E(x)$. Deleting every edge gives $\Gamma_\tau\equiv1$ for all $x$.

  2. Stalk excision weakly decreases the energy of the remaining data.

  3. Face deletion leaves $E_{\text{grad}}$ and $E_{\text{obs}}$ of any relational datum unchanged. It moves obstruction energy from curl to harmonic, i.e. from attributable to unattributable.

  4. On a 1-complex, $b_0-b_1=\sum_v\dim\mathcal F(v)-\sum_e\dim\mathcal F(e)$. Hence deleting an edge $e$ either lowers $b_1$, raises $b_0$, or both, by a total of $\dim\mathcal F(e)$.

Proof.

  1. Excision removes positive semidefinite terms from $L$, so $L'\preceq L$. Apply Proposition 5.6 to Γ_τ. For Γ_0, $\ker L\subseteq\ker L'$, and projection onto a larger subspace has a larger norm. With no edges, $L=0$.

  2. Removing a vertex removes its incident edge terms and leaves the other terms unchanged.

  3. This is Proposition 3.7 read in reverse.

  4. Rank–nullity applied to $\delta^0$ gives $b_0-b_1=\dim C^0-\dim C^1$. Deleting $e$ lowers $\dim C^1$ by $\dim\mathcal F(e)$. ∎ [E]

19.3 Readings

The theorem has four readings.

  1. Coherence by excision is always available. Any system can reach $\Gamma=1$ by deleting its relations. This is the formal reason the optimisation axiom N1 is a norm: measurement alone cannot forbid the efficient route to coherence. N1 forbids it because excision reduces structural diversity [D].
  2. Atomisation is perfect coherence. With every relation deleted, every individual is trivially consistent with itself and $b_0$ is maximal. This is the sheaf form of the atomised mass in Arendt’s account of totalitarian rule (Arendt 1951), read in The Totalitarian Sheaf (DRK-125) [D].
  3. Excision kills the invariant. By (iv), every deleted relation removes held difference ($b_1$ falls) or splits the system ($b_0$ rises). The Burnt Section (DRK-158) distinguishes “$H^1\to0$ via gluing” from “$H^1\to0$ via excision of stalks”. Theorem 19.2 makes the distinction computable [D].
  4. Deleting measurement converts fault into fate. By (iii), ceasing to measure the interior of a loop turns a local, attributable circulation into a systemic, unattributable class. Diffusion of responsibility has this exact form: the loss is unchanged, but no face can be held for it [D].

19.4 The admissibility rule

Definition 19.3 (Excision report). Every reported coherence change between $(X,\mathcal F)$ and $(X',\mathcal F')$ carries the excision vector $$\chi_{\text{exc}}=\big(\Delta\dim C^0,\ \Delta\dim C^1,\ \Delta\dim C^2;\ \Delta E;\ \Delta b_0,\ \Delta b_1\big),$$ together with the coherence recomputed on the common base [D].

Corollary 19.5 (Inversion filter, computable). A coherence gain is classified by recomputing it on the original base:

  • if it vanishes on the original base, it was produced by excision and is classified as a manufactured void (KO V.7);
  • if it persists, it was produced by gluing.

Proof. By Theorem 19.2(i), the remaining gain on a fixed base is attributable to changes of data or maps, not to the base. ∎ [E/D]

19.5 The anchored dual: compliance and Rayleigh monotonicity

Theorem 19.2 shows that an unanchored coherence measure rewards excision. Its dual shows that an anchored measure punishes it.

Proposition 19.6 (Rayleigh monotonicity; compliance). Let $f\in C^0$ be a declared load, and let $\varepsilon>0$. Define the compliance $$c_\varepsilon(f):=\big\langle f,(L+\varepsilon I)^{-1}f\big\rangle .$$ Then $c_\varepsilon(f)$ is non-decreasing under relation excision and non-increasing under adding relations.

As $\varepsilon\to0$ with $f\perp H^0$, $c_\varepsilon(f)\to\langle f,L^+f\rangle$. For the constant scalar sheaf and $f=\mathbf e_u-\mathbf e_v$, this limit is the effective resistance between $u$ and $v$. If excision disconnects the support of $f$, the compliance diverges [E].

Proof. Excision gives $L'\preceq L$, hence $L'+\varepsilon I\preceq L+\varepsilon I$. Inversion reverses the order. The effective-resistance identity is standard (Doyle & Snell 1984). ∎ [E]

For the constant scalar sheaf this is Rayleigh’s monotonicity law: cutting wires never lowers resistance (Doyle & Snell 1984) [E].

Compliance has a second meaning that ties it to measurement. When node values are estimated from noisy relative measurements, the variance of the estimated difference between $u$ and $v$ is proportional to the effective resistance between them (Barooah & Hespanha 2008). Excision therefore raises not only the cost of carrying a load but also the uncertainty with which the remaining system can know its own differences. Unanchored coherence goes up while the ability to verify it goes down [E/D].

Computed (institutional sheaf, unit load between H and C). The compliance is 2.667 on the full complex, 4.000 after deleting X–H, and infinite once F–H is also deleted and H is disconnected. Over the same deletions, the unanchored $\Gamma_\tau$ rose from 0.886 toward 1 (§19.2) [E].

Topology optimisation. Structural topology optimisation removes material deliberately. It minimises compliance $f^\top K(\varrho)^{-1}f$ subject to $K(\varrho)u=f$ and a volume bound $\int\varrho\le V^\ast$ (Bendsøe & Sigmund 2003). The Compartmentalized Manifold (DRK-161) applies this formulation to institutional design [E/D].

Engineering has long had the answer to totalisation:

  • Excision is permitted only under a load. The load $f$ is an anchor, and removing a member that carries load is penalised by the objective itself.
  • Without a load, the optimum is to remove everything, which is Theorem 19.2(i).

Corollary 19.7 (Anchored admissibility). A coherence intervention is admissible only if two conditions hold:

  • it passes Corollary 19.5 (the gain persists on the original base);
  • it does not raise the compliance $c_\varepsilon(f)$ for the declared loads $f$, i.e. for the demands the system exists to carry.

The first condition forbids hidden excision. The second forbids visible excision that sheds load-bearing relations [D].

Proof of consistency. By Proposition 19.6, any relation excision weakly raises compliance, and it raises it strictly when the deleted relation carries part of the load. ∎ [E]

24 Conclusion

What is proved. Model a multi-scale system as a cellular sheaf on a finite 2-complex. Then:

  • its relational data split uniquely into a part that local re-gauging removes, a topological part (ϰ) and a curvature part;
  • local relaxation removes exactly the first and stops exactly at the other two;
  • coherence of local states is measured by a resolvent quantity with a repair-cost meaning, sharp spectral bounds, the correct invariances, and a monotonicity under adding and deleting relations;
  • that monotonicity makes totalisation, coherence bought by deleting relations, always available. A norm, not a measurement, must forbid it.
  • the same excision raises compliance under any declared load (Rayleigh monotonicity), so anchored measures detect what unanchored ones reward;
  • obstructions reached by lifting across a cut and by integrating relational data are the same class (Proposition 3.11);
  • the repair behind Γ is Bayesian denoising, which ties coherence to Gaussian Markov random fields and to score-based generation (Propositions 5.12, 15.5);
  • trophic structure, input–output planning and iterated repair close onto the same linear algebra (Propositions 16.3–16.5).

What is computed. Every worked example, including corrected versions of v4.4’s two, is reproducible from a published script. Clock rings, U(1) rings, exchange complexes, toric codes, Kuramoto populations and signed triangles all instantiate the same three-part structure [E].

What is hypothesised. The same structure, applied to measured data from minds, groups, institutions and ecosystems, separates repairable from irreducible disagreement. Debt accrued by denying the irreducible part predicts the size of later corrections [H].
